## 0. What this brief delivers

**The oracle.** Every comparison brief in this series is gated against these designs, and brief 10
cannot choose a property tolerance without them — *"I don't have default property tolerances,
you'll have to create a design to test it"* (owner, 2026-09-21).

This brief is not documentation. It ships an example workspace because the example is the fixture,
and a fixture that only tests can see is one nobody maintains.

## 1. `R-lvs5-1` — the correct board, and why it is an attenuator

**`R-lvs5-1a`** A **3 dB resistive pi attenuator** on a 2-layer board: two ports, three resistors,
a ground pour on layer 2, stitching vias, and a bypass capacitor across nothing in particular so
there is a part the topology does not need.

**`R-lvs5-1b` Why this circuit.** It is the smallest design that is simultaneously: symmetric
(so the comparison must resolve a genuine automorphism rather than falling out of the names),
grounded (so brief 3's ground path is exercised on a drawn pour), multi-layer (so via bridging is
exercised), and boring enough that a human can verify the expected findings by reading it.
Something bigger would be a better demo and a worse oracle.

**`R-lvs5-1c`** The two shunt resistors are **identical and interchangeable**. That is deliberate:
matching them is an automorphism (note R-lvs-31 step 4), the pairing is arbitrary, and the report
must say `lvs.match.by-symmetry`. A design where every part is distinguishable would never
exercise the branch that is hardest to get right.

**`R-lvs5-1d`** Every component carries a footprint and a designator, and the layout was produced
by Update Layout, so every instance carries a `SchematicId`. **This is the easy path and it must
pass with zero findings** — not "zero errors", zero findings above info.

**`R-lvs5-1e` The artwork is GENERATED by a committed script**, `Attenuator.gen.py`, on
`Power Rail/Sensor board/layout/Board.gen.py`'s precedent. Hand-drawn artwork in a fixture is
artwork nobody can regenerate after a format change, and a fixture nobody can regenerate is one
that quietly stops meaning what it meant.

## 2. `R-lvs5-2` — the broken copy, and its six faults

**`R-lvs5-2a`** `Attenuator broken/` has the **identical schematic** — byte for byte, a copy — and
a layout produced by running `Attenuator.break.py` over the correct one. Each fault is one named,
minimal mutation, so a test can point at exactly one and a human can see it in the diff.

| Fault | The mutation | Expected finding |
|---|---|---|
| **F1 swapped net** | R2's right pad moved onto the wrong trace | `lvs.net.wrong` on R2 terminal 2, naming both nets |
| **F2 missing part** | C1's instance deleted; its pads left bare | `lvs.device.unmatched-schematic` for C1 |
| **F3 extra part** | a second, unwired R0402 placed | `lvs.device.unmatched-layout`, with its designator |
| **F4 short** | a 0.2 mm spur of copper joining IN and GND | `lvs.net.short`, **with the path and the spur's coordinate** |
| **F5 open** | one stitching via deleted, splitting the pour | `lvs.net.open` naming **three islands** and their pins |
| **F6 wrong value** | R3 re-pointed from 0402-100R to 0402-150R | `lvs.property.mismatch` — brief 10 |

**`R-lvs5-2b` Each fault is independently switchable.** `Attenuator.break.py --only F4` writes a
layout with exactly one fault, and the gate runs all six singly as well as all six together. Six
faults at once is the realistic case and also the one where a comparator that reports at the wrong
granularity looks fine.

**`R-lvs5-2c` The expected findings are asserted by DIAGNOSTIC ID and by the objects they name**,
never by their sentences. The id is the contract; the sentence is not.

**`R-lvs5-2d` F4 and F5 are the two that justify brief 2's merge edges and brief 8's islands.**
A short reported without its path and an open reported without its island structure are both
findings a user cannot act on, and these two faults are how we find out whether that work landed.

## 3. `R-lvs5-3` — the MMIC cell, and what only it can exercise

**`R-lvs5-3a`** `Bias tee/`: a spiral inductor PCell, a MIM capacitor PCell, a thin-film resistor
PCell, two RF pads and a backside-via ground, on the **shipped** `mmic-GaAs_2LM_100um` stackup.
The stackup is used unmodified — the point is partly to prove the starter technology works.

**`R-lvs5-3b` It is the only fixture that exercises the undrawn ground reference.** `Backside
Metal` has `DrawingLayers: []`, so every grounded terminal here reaches net `"0"` through brief
3's `R-lvs3-6c` and the run reports `lvs.ground.reference-undrawn` with a count. On a board fixture
this path is never taken.

**`R-lvs5-3c` It is the only fixture with parameters on the LAYOUT side.** A PCell carries
`PCellOrigin.Parameters` — resolved SI values — and `IsComputed` marks the ones the generator
derives from its own geometry. A board's land pattern claims nothing about resistance, so **brief
10's whole property comparison is unexercised without this cell.**

**`R-lvs5-3d` It is the only fixture with a MULTI-LAYER device.** The MIM cap's two plates are on
different conductors, joined by neither a via nor a touch, and its terminals are on different
layers. A layer-blind pin lookup passes every board test and fails here.

**`R-lvs5-3e` A broken variant is NOT shipped for the MMIC.** Its faults are produced in-test by
mutating the loaded model, because an MMIC fixture pair would double the artwork for faults that
are already covered structurally on the board. State this, so a later reader does not "fix" the
asymmetry.

## 4. `R-lvs5-4` — settling the property tolerances

This is the deliverable the owner asked for, and it is why brief 10 depends on this one.

**`R-lvs5-4a` The tolerances are MEASURED off `Bias tee/`, not chosen.** Procedure, recorded in
the brief's completion note:

1. Extract the cell. For each PCell device, take the schematic's requested value and the layout's
   resolved value.
2. Record the **actual spread** per unit dimension — resistance, capacitance, inductance, length,
   width, an integer count.
3. Set each default to a round number **comfortably above** the observed spread of a *correct*
   design and comfortably below the smallest fault anyone would want caught. Record both bounds.

**`R-lvs5-4b` Where the spread is zero, the tolerance is zero.** A derived parameter computed from
geometry by the same generator on both sides agrees exactly, and a non-zero tolerance there hides
a real class of error. Integers and enumerations are exact, always.

**`R-lvs5-4c` The measured numbers and the two bounds go in the completion note**, not only in the
code. A tolerance whose derivation is not written down is a magic number by the next release.

**`R-lvs5-4d` They are provisional and the note says so** — *"We can tweak it later"* (owner). They
live in one table, are overridable per technology (brief 10 `R-lvs10-3`), and every report prints
the tolerance it applied so a wrong default is visible rather than latent.

## 5. `R-lvs5-5` — it is a shipped example, not a test fixture

**`R-lvs5-5a`** A row in `examples/examples.json` and a `README.md`, so Tools ▸ Examples offers it.
Title: *"Layout versus schematic"*. The summary names both boards and says the broken one is
deliberately broken — **in the summary, not only inside the folder** — because an example that
looks like a mistake is an example someone reports as a bug.

**`R-lvs5-5b`** The README states what each fault is and what LVS says about it. That doubles as
the user documentation for reading a report, and brief 15 links to it rather than restating it.

**`R-lvs5-5c`** It obeys the example rules already in force: editable files, no absolute paths, no
personal paths, nothing the user cannot open and change.

**`R-lvs5-5d` `circuitRF_demo` is not used for any of this.** It is a scratchpad and several of
its benches report nonsense; a gate built on it would be a gate on unverified content.

## 6. Gate

`tests/Ui.Tests/Lvs/ProvingDesignTests.cs`. Most of it cannot run until brief 7; the parts that
can are written now and the rest are added as each brief lands, in this file.

1. **Both boards load, `check` clean.** The broken board is broken for *LVS*, not malformed — it
   must pass `check` with zero errors, or the fixture is testing the wrong thing (`R-lvs5-2a`).
2. **Every cell in all three designs has a terminal map, and its origin is `Declared` or
   `ImportTable`** — never `ByOrder` (brief 1). A fixture that relies on a positional guess cannot
   be an oracle for anything.
3. **The generator reproduces the artwork.** Run `Attenuator.gen.py` and compare the `.clay` byte
   for byte with the committed one (`R-lvs5-1e`).
4. **`Attenuator.break.py --only Fn` produces exactly one fault**, six times, each differing from
   the correct layout in a bounded, asserted way (`R-lvs5-2b`).
5. **The correct board compares with ZERO findings above info** (`R-lvs5-1d`) — added at brief 7.
6. **Each fault produces its expected finding, by id, naming the right objects** — six singly,
   then all six together (`R-lvs5-2a`, `c`) — added at briefs 7, 8, 10.
7. **The symmetric shunt pair reports `lvs.match.by-symmetry`** and the run is **deterministic**:
   ten runs, identical pairing and identical order (`R-lvs5-1c`) — added at brief 7.
8. **The MMIC cell reports `lvs.ground.reference-undrawn` with the correct via count**
   (`R-lvs5-3b`) — added at brief 7.
9. **The MIM cap's two terminals resolve on their own layers** (`R-lvs5-3d`) — added at brief 3,
   which is where a layer-blind lookup would pass everything else.
10. **The tolerance derivation is reproducible.** A test recomputes the spread of `R-lvs5-4a` and
    asserts each shipped default sits between the two recorded bounds — so a later PCell change
    that widens the spread past the tolerance **fails here**, loudly, instead of silently making
    LVS pass a real error (`R-lvs5-4c`) — added at brief 10.
11. **The examples row resolves and the folder opens** through the same path Tools ▸ Examples uses
    (`R-lvs5-5a`).

## 7. Scope

- **Three designs, not a library.** More fixtures are a later brief's problem if a real gap appears.
- **No MMIC broken variant on disk** (`R-lvs5-3e`).
- **No modification to any existing example.** These are new folders.
- **No tolerance is invented before it is measured** (`R-lvs5-4a`).

## 8. Completion note (2026-09-21)

Built. `examples/LVS/` ships, `examples/examples.json` offers it as *Layout versus schematic*, and
`tests/Ui.Tests/Lvs/ProvingDesignTests.cs` is the gate — 22 tests, all passing, with the
comparison half (gates 5, 6, 7) still to be added to that same file as briefs 7, 8 and 10 land.

**Everything worth knowing is in `examples/RESOLVED.md`**, including §4's measured tolerances and
their two bounds; `src/Design/RESOLVED.md` carries the one finding that is about code rather than
about the fixture. What follows is only the map.

**Four findings, in the order they matter.**

1. **A `Net` stamp on copper that touches unnamed copper is reported as a zero-clearance spacing
   violation** — on correct artwork, on any board with footprints on it. `examples/Power Rail`
   misses it only because its technology declares no rules at all. Not fixed (the series changes
   no DRC behaviour); the fixture stamps only the ground pour, which is the one shape on its layer.
2. **`DeviceType.CouldBe` vetoes the ordinary board flow**: an Update-Layout placement reads as
   `DeviceKind.Cell` and its schematic component as `DeviceKind.Resistor`, and the two are refused.
   Brief 7 must resolve this before gate 5 can pass; two small candidate fixes are written down.
3. **A kit-generated PCell cell has no symbol view and therefore no terminal map**, so brief 1's
   R-lvs1-5b is unimplemented and every placed kit part is unmatchable today. The MMIC fixture's
   parts are committed cells carrying a `PCellOrigin` for that reason, and they are what will
   prove the writer when it lands.
4. **F5 produces two ground islands and not three** — a via joins at most one piece per conductor,
   so on two layers it is an edge of degree two and one deletion splits a tree in two. §2's table
   is corrected here, in the generator's own comment and in the example's README.

**Four deliberate departures from §0's sketch, each for a reason.**

- **The MMIC technology keeps its shipped NAME**, `mmic-GaAs_2LM_100um.ctech`, rather than being
  renamed `mmic-2lm-lvs.ctech`. R-lvs5-3a wants it unmodified and a renamed copy invites the
  question of whether it was edited; a test asserts byte identity against
  `src/Design/resources/technologies/`.
- **There is no `TERM` footprint.** A placement carrying a designator is a DEVICE, so two TERM
  placements would be two layout devices the schematic's `Pin` markers deliberately are not. The
  two ports are boundary PINS on the root `.clay`, resolved through the terminal map.
- **The board's parts are named after what they are** (`R0402-294R`, not `R0402`), because a land
  pattern is the only place a board layout can state a value and F6 needs one to state. The parts
  are still one shared 0402 land pattern geometrically. `R0402-150R` exists solely to be F6's
  wrong part.
- **A fourth cell, `Attenuator bench`, was added.** Every shipped example must have something to
  run (`ExampleWorkspacesTests`), and a cell with `Pin` ports has no analysis it could carry. The
  bench also earns its place: it reads −2.97 dB at 0.1 GHz rising to −2.55 dB at 6 GHz, which is
  C1 shunting R2 — the part the topology does not need, measured.

**One thing the fixture forced that is not a departure:** `Attenuator` needed a `symbol/` view
before the bench could place it. Without one, `CellSymbolResolver` finds no `.csym`, the
instance's pins fall back to placeholder geometry, the wires reach nothing, and the extraction
succeeds with the DUT simply absent — S21 = 0 dB across the band. The symbol's pin names are the
terminal map's own, so the cell would derive the same map by name even with no block declared.
