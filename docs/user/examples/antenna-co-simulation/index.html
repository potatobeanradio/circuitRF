Antenna Co-Simulation

A meander antenna on a 45 x 95 mm board, solved once at five frequencies with each of its parts as a port. An EM Field Data block then gives S11, the radiation pattern and realized gain from a schematic, so the optimizer matches it and a sweep shows an inductor's error with no new EM run.

A meander antenna on a 45 × 95 mm board, solved once with each of its parts as a port. From then on a schematic gives S11, the radiation pattern and realized gain for any part values, in under a second and with no new EM run.

The two cells

Meander Antenna. One copper layer on 1.6 mm FR-4: a 43 × 62 mm ground, a 1 mm coplanar feed in a slot in the ground, and a 2 mm wide meander about 98 mm long above it. Three 0402 land patterns sit where the feed meets the antenna:

Part Where it goes
C1 shunt, feed to ground
L1 series, feed to antenna
C2 shunt, antenna to ground, not fitted (Disable = Open)

Its schematic is the board's circuit: the source, C1 and L1, with C2 drawn but not fitted. The layout labels P1 where the feed starts. A 3D view does not take port labels from a layout it places, so the 3D view has a P1 of its own, a lumped sheet across the 0.5 mm gap at the end of the slot.

Its 3D view has one setup, Pattern: EMerge at the Accurate preset, 828 to 908 MHz in 20 MHz steps, with Component ports on every part and Radiation pattern on. It keeps a far field at each of the five frequencies.

Antenna Bench (a test bench). The saved run as one EM Field Data block, EM1, on results/Meander Antenna Pattern.emerge.cemr, with the parts drawn around it. Each part stands from its port's pin to ground, because a gap port's own pad is that port's reference. The values come from the variables Lser, dL and Cshunt.

Analysis What it does
SP1 S11 from 828 to 908 MHz
F1 the pattern at 868 MHz, for the goals and the first tab
F2 the pattern at every stored frequency
F3, swept by F3_sweep_dL the pattern at 868 MHz with the inductor 2 nH low to 2 nH high

Run it

  1. Simulate the Meander Antenna's 3D view (setup Pattern). It needs EMerge, and the first run offers to install it. On a 10-core Mac with 17 GB it took 2 min 45 s, peaking at 9.4 GB, on 81,547 tetrahedra.
  2. Simulate the Antenna Bench, then open its Data Display.
  3. Optimize. The Optimizer panel already holds the variables, two goals and an algorithm (simplex, seed 1). Press Run, apply its best values and simulate again to redraw the plots. Moving a tuning slider on Lser or Cshunt redraws the pattern as you go.

What you will see

As drawn, with 10 nH and 2.7 pF, the antenna is badly mismatched. The optimizer meets both goals in 3 iterations and 9 evaluations, in under a second:

At 868 MHz As drawn Best
Lser 10 nH 2.57 nH
Cshunt 2.7 pF 3.99 pF
Match: S11, ≤ −15 dB −3.22 dB −16.9 dB
Gain: realized gain, ≥ 1.5 dBi −0.77 dBi 2.06 dBi
Gain 2.04 dBi 2.15 dBi
Directivity 2.21 dBi 2.28 dBi

The parts decide how much power gets in, and barely change the shape. Directivity moves by 0.07 dB between the two columns while realized gain moves by 2.8 dB. The tabs of the Data Display show this three ways, and these numbers are for the best values:

  • Match and pattern. S11 is below −9.5 dB from 858 to 894 MHz. The pattern is a donut around the board's long axis, with a shallow null along it.
  • Across the band. Realized gain is −0.61, 1.02, 2.06, 1.94 and 0.71 dBi from 828 to 908 MHz, while the cuts stay nearly on top of each other.
  • Inductor error. With L1 2 nH low to 2 nH high, realized gain is 1.03, 1.67, 2.06, 2.12 and 1.85 dBi. The best point on this curve is not 0 nH, since the optimizer stopped once both goals were met.

The curves on the last two tabs come from the stored results. A frequency between the five stored ones has S but no pattern, so F1 and F3 must name one of the five, and F2 takes them all with freq=all.

Checked against Palace

The same board at 868 MHz, on Palace at second order, 74,472 tetrahedra. It took 9 min 26 s for this one frequency, with other work running on the machine.

The two agree on the match and on the part values that reach it. EMerge's gain reads about 0.6 dB above Palace's and its efficiency about 8 to 10 points higher, so read this example's gain as showing how the parts move it, rather than as a value to design to. To compare for yourself, set the setup's solver to Palace.

What was traded for speed

  • One copper layer and no vias. Stitching vias set the size of a mesh long before the wavelength does, so leaving them out is most of the saving. A two-layer board of the same outline with 324 vias took 7 min 12 s at one frequency in Palace.
  • Parts are ideal, and their bodies are not in the 3D model.
  • The air box is 170 mm on every face, about λ/2. On such a board gain and efficiency keep changing as the box grows, even after S11 and directivity have settled, so compare a pattern's gain at a second box size before relying on it. EMerge's far field also reads high when the box is close.
  • Nothing is compared with a measurement. This board was never built, so the numbers show the method rather than a design to copy.
