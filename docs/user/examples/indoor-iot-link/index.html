Indoor IoT Link

An 868 MHz FSK sensor two floors from its gateway through a Propagation Channel: ITU-R P.1238 office loss with floors and walls, a tapped delay line read from the office's power-delay profile file, received power and outage over 2,000 drops, and bit errors counted over 50.

A battery sensor sends 100 kb/s FSK at 868.95 MHz to a gateway two floors up. Will it get through? A Propagation Channel answers as a percentage: each run is one drop, one realization of the shadowing and the fading, and a sweep over drop is the Monte Carlo. Both benches share the same link:

Part What it is
SENSOR A MOD_Source playing 2-FSK, ±45 kHz deviation, PN9 (511 bits, one 5.11 ms record), at 10 dBm
CH1 ITU-R P.1238 office without line of sight (PathLoss=P1238 Environment=office Los=nlos), 28 m, Floors=2, and a concrete wall and two partitions on the path (Partitions="10,6,6", dB each)
Multipath from the office's own power-delay profile, profiles/office_pdp.csv: six taps over 110 ns (Multipath=file), each Rayleigh-fading at walking pace (Fading=rayleigh Velocity=1, 1 m/s)
LNA, GW The gateway: a 2 dBi antenna (RxGain=2), a 20 dB, 6 dB NF front end and its 50 Ω input

The sensitivity is −98 dBm, Sens: on the second bench the FSK detector's bit errors pass 1 % below about −100 dBm, and −98 keeps 2 dB in hand.

1 Coverage

An S-parameter run swept over 2,000 drops. Each drop's received power is the sensor's 10 dBm plus the channel's gain through that drop, SP1.link("CH1", "Gain_dB"), with the path loss, both antennas, the shadowing and the fading in it.

Measure Value
Path loss, P.1238 + two floors + partitions 104.3 dB (60.2 free space, 3.1 the office's excess, 19 two floors, 22 the walls)
Median received power PrxMedian_dBm −94.2 dBm
Outage, the drops below −98 dBm 29.1 %, its exact 95 % interval 27.1 to 31.1 %

The median is only 3.8 dB above the sensitivity, and the office's shadowing (σ = 5 dB) and the Rayleigh fading together need about 20 dB for 99 %: two floors and three walls are too much for this sensor. Prx_cdf draws the distribution. Move the gateway one floor down (Floors=1, 9 dB less) or raise the sensor's power, and run it again. The whole sweep takes two seconds.

2 Bit Errors

The same link in an envelope run with Noise=yes, over drops 1 to 50: the FSK record is demodulated at Irx and every bit decided. BER is each drop's counted bit error rate, Prx_dBm its received power (the same drops as the first bench's), and BERmean their average.

Measure Value
Drops with any bit error 14 of 50
Drops above 1 % 12 of 50, every one below −100 dBm
BERmean 0.026
Outage of the received power below −98 dBm 30 % (17.9 to 44.6 %)

Plot BER against Prx_dBm and the sensitivity is where the errors start. The run's notes say what regime the link is in: f_D = 2.9 Hz, so the 5.11 ms record sees a frozen channel (f_D·T = 0.015) and every drop's statistics come from other drops; the 18 ns delay spread gives an 11 MHz coherence bandwidth, against a signal 290 kHz wide, so the office fades it flat.

What was traded for run time

The envelope bench reads 50 drops (3 s); 2,000 would take two minutes and would only resolve the bit error rate the coverage bench already gives as an outage. Noise=yes keeps the run from replaying the sensor's waves between drops, so each drop solves both ends.
