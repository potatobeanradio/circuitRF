PCB Component Ports

A 50 ohm line with a three-part pi low-pass filter, solved once with each part as a component port, on Planar MoM, Palace and EMerge. A test bench puts the parts back and lays the three answers over the schematic alone; change a part value and no EM run is needed.

A 50 Ω microstrip line on a two-layer board with a three-part pi low-pass filter on it: a shunt capacitor, a series inductor and a second shunt capacitor. The board is solved once with each part as a component port: a port across the part's own pads that you never draw. From then on the parts live in a schematic. Change a value and the response changes in a fraction of a second, with no new EM run.

The board is solved three ways, on the Planar MoM solver, on Palace and on EMerge, and a test bench lays the three answers over the schematic alone.

How the board was built

These are the steps to copy when you build your own.

  1. Schematic first. Filter Board's schematic is the circuit: two 100 mil MLIN lines, C1 and C2 to ground and L1 in series, with IN and OUT pins. Each part's Footprint is smt:0603.
  2. Design ▸ Update Layout from Schematic. It places the lines and the land patterns, drops a ground via beside each capacitor's ground pad, and pours the ground plane on Bottom Copper under them. C2 was then moved to the same side of the line as C1, so the two ground vias sit 2.4 mm apart. The Planar solver needs vias within a tenth of a wavelength of each other at the top of the sweep, which is 5 mm at 6 GHz. A top-side ground bar joins the two via pads.
  3. Ports where the signal enters and leaves. P1 and P2 are ordinary edge ports drawn at the two line ends. Nothing is drawn for the three parts.
  4. LVS. circuitrf lvs "Filter Board" matches. Each board pin sits on a short launch drawn over its line end, so the pin has copper of its own.
  5. The EM setup, Filter Board Planar (Filter Board/em). Under Component ports, C1, L1 and C2 are ticked. Check builds each one before anything is meshed and posts its verdict to Messages. With the setup open, the layout shows each component port as a dashed gap mark with its number: 3, 4 and 5, after the two drawn ports.
  6. Design ▸ New 3D View from Layout. The 3D view places the board's layout and brings its component ports with it, as a 3D setup. That setup was copied once more, so the view has two: Palace and EMerge.

Run it

Step What Time on a 10-core Mac
1 Simulate the EM setup Filter Board Planar about 2.5 min
2 Open Filter Board's 3D view, run setup Palace about 3.5 min
3 Run setup EMerge in the same view about 10 s
4 Simulate Bench, then open Bench.cdd under a second

The first 3D run offers to install its solver. Each run also makes a cell named after it, such as Filter Board Planar_cosim: the board's result with each part across its own port. It is ready to simulate, so you can look at one solver's answer before the others have run. The Bench reads all three results, so run all three before simulating it.

What you will see

Bench.cdd plots |S21| four ways:

Schematic only Planar MoM Palace EMerge
−3 dB 1.98 GHz 1.67 GHz 1.70 GHz 1.70 GHz
Deepest point none below 6 GHz 3.75 GHz, −25.3 dB 3.75 GHz, −22.5 dB 3.8 GHz, −22.3 dB
|S21| at 2 GHz −3.1 dB −7.0 dB −6.3 dB −6.4 dB
|S21| at 6 GHz −28.1 dB −17.2 dB −14.8 dB −14.7 dB

The schematic alone gets this filter wrong. It knows nothing about the board. The pads add capacitance, so the cutoff falls from 1.98 to 1.70 GHz. Each shunt capacitor reaches ground through its pad, its via and the ground bar, and that path's inductance resonates with the capacitor at about 3.8 GHz. That is the notch, and above it the stopband rises again where the schematic says it keeps falling. All three EM solvers show it.

Palace and EMerge agree within 0.4 dB at every frequency.

Planar MoM follows them to within 0.7 dB up to the cutoff and reads 2 to 3 dB deeper in the stopband. Most of that difference sits at the two edge ports, which the two kinds of solver model differently. Planar removes its edge port's own effect back to the end of the line. In 3D, the port is a lumped sheet from the line down to the ground plane, and its height stays in the answer. Treat Planar and 3D as two models of the board, not as one checking the other.

Things to try

  • Change a part. Edit Cshunt or Lseries in the Bench's VAR block and simulate again. All four curves move, and no EM run is needed. Tuning and the Optimizer work on these variables the same way.
  • Make it an attenuator. Replace the three parts on an EM result with resistors, 292 Ω, 17.6 Ω and 292 Ω for a 3 dB pi pad. The board's EM result is the same one, because the parts are not in it.
  • Turn a part around. A component port follows its part. Move or rotate L1 in the layout, and the next run, Check or mark uses its new place. The setup is not edited.

What was traded for speed

  • Planar: Edge mesh off. With it on, the mesh has about five times the unknowns and the run takes several times as long. On this board it moves |S| by at most 0.015.
  • Palace: one adaptive pass (AdaptiveMaxIterations 0, inside the setup's Palace settings). The default three passes took over 30 minutes.
  • The air box is stated: 5 mm on the top and sides of both 3D setups, about 10 times the substrate height. The default puts the top face one board-thickness above the copper, inside the line's fringing field, where it absorbs power that is not really lost. With it, Palace read |S11| −0.84 dB at 6 GHz where the 5 mm box reads −0.29 dB.
  • The parts are ideal, and their bodies are not in any of the EM models.
