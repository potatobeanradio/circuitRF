S-Parameter Noise

Noise=yes on an S-parameter run: a 6 dB pad whose noise figure follows its temperature, a cascade of two amplifiers and a pad laid over Friis' formula, and a noise-matched LNA with its optimum source and two constant-noise-figure circles on a Smith chart. Every number is checked against a closed form.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ S-Parameter Noise copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

Open a numbered bench, Simulate, and its Data Display opens on the result. The three run in a second each, in any order:

  • 1 Attenuator: a matched 6 dB pad, its temperature swept from 20 K to 400 K. Its noise figure is 6 dB at 290 K and 1 + (T/T0)(L − 1) at every other temperature.
  • 2 Friis Cascade: a low-noise stage, a pad and a second stage. The run's noise figure lies on Friis' formula at every pad loss from 1 to 10 dB.
  • 3 LNA Noise Circles: a transistor-like two-port behind an input noise match, with a stabilising resistor. NF against NFmin, the noise temperatures, the optimum source on a Smith chart and as an impedance, and two circles of constant noise figure.

Each bench's S-parameter analysis has Noise ticked (Noise=yes in the netlist). That adds the noise-wave correlation Cs to the run, and for a two-port the noise figure and noise parameters as cubes beside S: NF and NFmin (dB), Fn and Fmin (the same, linear), Te and Tmin (the noise temperatures, K), Rn (Ω), and the optimum source Sopt, also as an impedance Zopt. They take the names commonly used for them. Every part in these benches is noisy: the resistors and the lossy L and C are thermal at their own temperature, and each SnP block is noisy by the noise data in its file. So no run warns about a noiseless part. The full description is in Reference ▸ Simulations ▸ Small-signal noise.

Running a bench

  1. File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ S-Parameter Noise copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it.
  2. In the Workspace tree, open a bench's schematic, for example 1 Attenuator's.
  3. Simulate.
  4. The bench's Data Display, 1 Attenuator.cdd beside the .cws, opens on the new result. It is in the tree too, to open again later.

To see the noise switch, open the bench's SP1 from the Analyses panel: the Noise box is under the frequency plan.

1 Attenuator

A π pad of three resistors between 50 Ω ports. Its loss is the variable L_dB = 6, and the resistors follow from it:

K = 10^(L_dB/20)      Rsh = 50·(K + 1)/(K − 1) = 150.48 Ω      Rse = 50·(K² − 1)/(2K) = 37.35 Ω

temp = 16.85 is the design's ambient in °C, which is 290 K. A parametric sweep SW1 steps temp from −253.15 °C to 126.85 °C, which is 20 K to 400 K in 10 K steps, around one frequency, 1 GHz. A pad is flat, so one is enough.

A passive part's noise is the thermal noise of its own loss, at its own temperature. For a matched pad at temperature T with loss L (a ratio), that gives

F = 1 + (T/T0)·(L − 1)          T0 = 290 K

At T = T0 the noise figure is the loss. The MEAS1 block writes this formula as NF_closed, beside the run's NF:

Pad temperature NF, circuitRF NF_closed
20 K 0.812 dB 0.812 dB
80 K 2.606 dB 2.606 dB
290 K 6.000 dB 6.000 dB
300 K, circuitRF's default ambient 6.111 dB 6.111 dB
400 K 7.086 dB 7.086 dB

They agree to 1e-14 dB at all 39 temperatures. What the display shows (1 Attenuator.cdd):

  • Noise figure of a 6 dB pad against its temperature: the run's NF as dots, NF_closed as a line through them, and the loss, −dB(S21), dashed at 6 dB. The two cross at 290 K, where the marker reads 6.000 dB.
  • Noise temperature of the wave leaving port 2: Tn = Re Cs(2,2)/k, against Bosma's T(1 − 1/L) for a passive network. At 290 K both are 217.2 K.

A design that states no temperature runs at 26.85 °C, 300 K, not at T0, and there this pad reads 6.111 dB. Noise figures are referred to 290 K, so a passive part 10 K warmer than that makes a little more noise than its loss. Write temp = 16.85 in a design whose passive parts should be at T0.

2 Friis Cascade

Port 1, stage A, a matched π pad, stage B, port 2, all matched to 50 Ω. Both stages are SnP blocks whose files carry a Touchstone noise block:

Stage File Gain Noise data NF from 50 Ω
A, low-noise data/amp_a.s2p 16 dB NFmin 0.8 dB at Γopt 0.25∠45°, rn 0.25 0.957 dB
Pad three resistors at 290 K −L_dB its loss L_dB
B data/amp_b.s2p 12 dB NFmin 3 dB at Γopt 0, rn 0.4 3 dB

SW1 sweeps the pad's loss L_dB from 1 to 10 dB. Friis' formula for three matched stages is

F = FA + (L − 1)/GA + (FB − 1)·L/GA

where FA is stage A's noise factor from a 50 Ω source. Stage A's optimum source is not 50 Ω, so FA is above its Fmin:

FA = Fmin + 4·rn·|Γopt|² / |1 + Γopt|²       (Γs = 0)

The VAR block holds each stage's numbers as its file states them (GA_dB, NFminA_dB, GoptA, rnA, GB_dB, NFB_dB). MEAS1 turns them into FA, GA, FB, and NF_friis is the formula above.

Pad NF, circuitRF NF_friis NFmin, circuitRF Pad first, NF_padfirst
1 dB 1.087 dB 1.087 dB 0.943 dB 2.043 dB
3 dB 1.210 dB 1.210 dB 1.078 dB 4.043 dB
10 dB 2.362 dB 2.362 dB 2.296 dB 11.043 dB

The run and the formula agree to 3e-14 dB at every loss and at each of the four frequencies, 1 to 4 GHz. The gain is GA − L + GB to 2e-13 dB. What the display shows (2 Friis Cascade.cdd):

  • Cascade noise figure against the pad's loss: the run's NF as dots on NF_friis, and the run's NFmin dashed below it. NFmin is lower because stage A would rather see a source of 0.25∠45° than 50 Ω. The marker is on the 3 dB pad.
  • Why the low-noise stage goes first: the same cascade, against NF_padfirst, Friis' formula with the pad ahead of stage A, L·FA + (FB − 1)·L/GA. A loss in front of the first gain adds to the noise figure decibel for decibel; behind 16 dB of gain, the 3 dB pad costs 0.17 dB over the 1.04 dB of the two stages alone.
  • Cascade gain: the run's gain on GA − L + GB.

The pad is at 290 K (temp = 16.85), which is what makes its noise factor equal to its loss. Noise data from a file is absolute and does not move with the ambient.

3 LNA Noise Circles

data/transistor.s2p is a synthetic transistor-like two-port from 2 to 6 GHz with a noise block. At 4 GHz it has NFmin 0.65 dB at Γopt 0.65∠75° and rn 0.19. It is not unconditionally stable below about 4 GHz. The bench builds a narrow-band LNA around it:

  • Input noise match: a shunt C1 (SRC, 0.43 pF with 0.25 Ω of ESR) and a series L1 (SRL, 3.16 nH with 2 Ω, a Q of 40 at 4 GHz). They were tuned so that the LNA's own optimum source is 50 Ω at 4 GHz.
  • Stabilising resistor: Rstab, 300 Ω from the output to ground. With it, Rollett's K is 1.27 to 1.64 across the band. Without it, K falls to 1.01 at 3 GHz, which leaves no margin; the transistor on its own is at 0.92 there.

SP1 runs from 3 to 5 GHz in 50 MHz steps. MEAS1 adds the gain, K_rollett = K(SP1.S), and two noise circles, NF_125 = ns_circle(SP1.S, 1.25) and NF_200 = ns_circle(SP1.S, 2). Each circle is a cube over frequency and 101 points: the source reflections that give that noise figure.

At 4 GHz
NF from 50 Ω 0.979 dB
NFmin 0.979 dB, at an Sopt within 0.002 of the centre
Te and Tmin 73.4 K, both
Zopt 50.08 − j0.13 Ω
Rn 5.0 Ω
Gain 11.4 dB
K 1.43

The LNA's NFmin is 0.33 dB above the transistor's. Most of that, 0.28 dB, is the loss in L1 and C1: with their resistances set to 0, NFmin is 0.70 dB. Rstab's own noise is the other 0.06 dB.

What the display shows (3 LNA Noise Circles.cdd):

  • Noise figure of the LNA: NF from 50 Ω against NFmin, 3 to 5 GHz. They touch at 4 GHz, where the match puts 50 Ω at the optimum, and part on either side. Below 4 GHz the NF is lower still, since NFmin falls faster than the mismatch grows.
  • Sopt and two noise circles: Sopt's locus from 3 to 5 GHz, passing through the centre of the chart at 4 GHz (the marker), and the 1.25 dB and 2 dB circles at 4 GHz, about 0.27 dB and 1 dB above NFmin. The circle traces are NF_125[20, :] and NF_200[20, :]: point 20 of the frequency axis is 4 GHz. NF_125[~, :] draws one circle per frequency instead.
  • Gain and Stability: dB(S21), and K above 1 everywhere.
  • Equivalent input noise temperature: Te from 50 Ω against Tmin, T0·(F − 1) and T0·(Fmin − 1) with T0 = 290 K. They meet at 73.4 K at 4 GHz (the marker), as NF and NFmin do; a noise temperature reads small differences at a low noise figure more plainly than decibels.
  • Optimum source impedance Zopt: Sopt as an impedance, Re and Im, from 33 + j32 Ω at 3 GHz to 137 Ω at 5 GHz. The marker reads 50.08 Ω at 4 GHz, the port's own 50 Ω.

Every point of both circles gives its own noise figure back through nf(SP1.S, Γs), to 3e-15 dB.

The data

Every file in data/ is synthetic, written for this example, and says what it is in its header. None is a real part.

File What it is
data/amp_a.s2p Stage A: matched and unilateral, 16 dB, NFmin 0.8 dB at Γopt 0.25∠45°, rn 0.25, flat from 1 to 4 GHz
data/amp_b.s2p Stage B: matched and unilateral, 12 dB, NFmin 3 dB at Γopt 0, rn 0.4, flat from 1 to 4 GHz
data/transistor.s2p A transistor-like two-port, 2 to 6 GHz in 0.5 GHz steps; every S and noise value runs linearly between the end values in its header

The noise block is Touchstone 1.x: after the S data, one row per frequency of f NFmin(dB) |Γopt| ∠Γopt(°) rn, with rn = Rn/R on the option line's R. Between rows the noise parameters are linear.

To keep a run's noise as a file, export S as Touchstone, or from a terminal:

circuitrf sparam "3 LNA Noise Circles/schematic/3 LNA Noise Circles.csch" -o lna.s2p

The .s2p it writes carries the noise block, so the LNA can be placed as an SnP in a bigger design with its noise.
