Class-E with a Transient Seed

A 1 GHz class-E stage solved by harmonic balance started from a transient run (InitialGuess=transient), beside a transient bench showing the drain waveforms the start came from. 31.4 dBm at 88 % drain efficiency.

A 1 GHz class-E power amplifier, solved by harmonic balance started from a transient run (InitialGuess=transient), beside a transient bench that shows the waveforms that start came from. Both benches carry the same stage.

The stage

A FET (the built-in Angelov law, Ipk = 0.5 A) driven hard as a switch: a 30 dBm, 1 GHz P1Tone into its gate through CIN, the gate biased at −2.5 V through the choke LG, with the gate diode DG clamping the drive. The drain is fed from 5 V through the choke LD, past the IProbe IDD. C1 (2.92 pF) is the class-E shunt capacitance; L0–C0 (7.96 nH, 4.13 pF) are the series tank with the class-E excess reactance, into the 10 Ω load RL through the IProbe IOUT. DB is the drain's body diode. These are the class-E values for 10 Ω at 1 GHz. The ideal stage delivers 0.577 · 5² / 10 = 1.44 W (31.6 dBm) at 100 % efficiency.

RL is a Term of 10 Ω. Outside S-parameters a Term is a load of its Z.

ClassE_HB

Harmonic balance at 1 GHz with MaxHarm=31 and InitialGuess=transient. Before Newton starts, circuitRF simulates the stage in time from its DC point at full drive until one period looks like the next, and hands the last period's spectrum to Newton. The run's message says how that went:

harmonic balance started from a transient seed: 43 periods, settled; no choke or block scaled;
harmonic balance converged in 5 Newton iterations from it.
Measure Result
Pout_dBm 31.42 dBm
Pdc_W 1.579 W
DE_pct 87.9 % drain efficiency

The run also writes Seed.Periods (43), Seed.Settled (1) and Seed.HbIterations (5). The answer is harmonic balance's own, to its own tolerance: the transient run only supplies where Newton starts.

Against the default start. Set InitialGuess back to dc and the same answer is reached from the DC point. With DriveStepping=Never it takes 55 Newton iterations, and a MaxIter of 54 or less does not converge at all. The default DriveStepping=IfNecessary gets there too, by stepping the drive up. A switching stage is one of the circuits harmonic balance finds hardest to start, because its devices only conduct as a switch at full drive. The seed is worth reaching for when a DC start fails or stalls, and InitialGuess=fallback tries the DC start first and seeds only if it fails.

Fast bias. SeedFastBias=yes (the default) scales, in the transient run only, any RF choke whose reactance is more than 50 times the impedance it sees, and any DC block whose reactance is less than a fiftieth of it, because those would keep the transient run going for thousands of periods. Here none qualifies, as the message says, and the run settles in 43 periods unaided. Put a 10 µH choke in place of LD and it would be scaled, and named in the message.

ClassE_Transient

The same stage simulated for 60 ns, 60 periods, from its DC point. Plot V(d) (the drain) and I(IOUT) together: the drain voltage swings up to about 19 V while the switch is open and falls back to zero before it closes, which is the class-E condition. The peak is near the ideal 3.56 × 5 V = 17.8 V. The first 40 ns or so are the start-up the seed watched settle.

The two benches agree on the output power to 0.002 dB.

Settings traded for run time

MaxHarm. The drain current switches in a fraction of a period, and harmonic balance needs harmonics to follow it. At MaxHarm=31, efficiency agrees with a transient run at a 1 ps step to 0.06 pp. At 15, the run is no faster here and the efficiency reads 0.8 pp low:

MaxHarm DE_pct Pdc_W Pout_dBm
15 87.13 % 1.592 W 31.422 dBm
31 (shipped) 87.90 % 1.579 W 31.422 dBm
63 87.97 % 1.578 W 31.423 dBm

The transient bench's step. Step=5p, 200 points a period. At 1 ps the efficiency reads 87.96 % instead of 87.94 %, for five times the steps.
