Receiver Noise

The noise work of a 2.45 GHz receiver, bench by bench: a FET LNA noise-matched with its circles and which part sets its noise, the chain's budget against Friis, a diode mixer's SSB and DSB noise figure against LO drive, the LNA under a blocker, a noise sourcepull under drive, an NR signal's EVM through the chain with a phase-noisy LO, and the VCO whose phase noise that LO carries.

Open this example

Open a numbered bench, Simulate, and its Data Display opens on the result. Run them in order: each one builds on the one before it.

  1. 1 LNA Noise Match: a one-stage FET LNA, noise-matched at 2.45 GHz. NF, NFmin, the noise and gain circles, and which part sets the noise. Under a second.
  2. 2 Receiver Budget: the receiver as system blocks, LNA to IF amplifier. Its NF lies on Friis' formula at every LNA gain. Under a second.
  3. 3 Mixer Noise: a single-diode mixer. SSB and DSB noise figure against LO drive, and how much of the noise comes in through the image. Under a second.
  4. 4 LNA Under a Blocker: bench 1's LNA with a 2.3 GHz blocker in front of it. NF against blocker power. Under a second.
  5. 5 Noise Sourcepull: bench 1's transistor stage on a source-tuner grid. NF and gain contours on the source plane, at two drive levels. Two seconds.
  6. 6 Receiver EVM: an NR signal through bench 2's chain, with a real mixer and a phase-noisy LO. EVM against input power, split into noise and distortion. About twenty seconds.
  7. 7 VCO Phase Noise: the LO itself, a varactor-tuned Colpitts VCO. Its tuning curve and phase noise. Bench 6's LO carries this VCO's phase noise. Two seconds.

Every bench sets temp = 16.85, which puts the whole design at 290 K, the temperature a noise figure is referred to. A design that states no temperature runs at 300 K, and its passive parts are then a little noisier than their loss. circuitrf check on this workspace reports that setting once per bench, as a warning, and nothing else.

The noise conventions, and which analysis computes what, are in Reference ▸ Simulations ▸ Noise.

1 LNA Noise Match

A Statz-model FET at −0.5 V gate and 3 V drain, with Ri = 3 Ω, Tg = 300 K and Td = 3000 K. That is the two-temperature noise model: the intrinsic gate resistance is thermal at the gate temperature, and the output conductance is thermal at the drain temperature. Beside it are the parts a real stage has:

  • Rg (1 Ω) and Rs (0.5 Ω): extrinsic gate and source resistance, thermal at 290 K.
  • LS (1 nH): source degeneration.
  • Rd (10 Ω) and Rstab (82 Ω to ground): the stabilising network at the drain.
  • L1 (5.7 nH, 2.19 Ω, a Q of 40) and C1 (0.56 pF): the input noise match. They were tuned so that the LNA's own optimum source is 50 Ω at 2.45 GHz.

SP1 runs from 1.5 to 3.5 GHz with Noise ticked.

At 2.45 GHz
NF from 50 Ω 0.618 dB
NFmin the same, to 0.001 dB: Sopt is within 0.02 of the chart's centre
Gain 11.2 dB
Rollett K above 2 across the whole sweep

Which part sets the noise (NFpct, each part's share of F − 1):

Part Share
F1, the transistor 34.1 %
Rstab 30.4 %
L1, the match inductor's loss 28.7 %
Rg 3.5 %
Rs, Rd under 2 % each

The stabilising resistor costs as much as the transistor. It sits at the drain, after only one stage of gain, so its thermal noise is not divided down much. A resistor further down the chain would cost less, and a lossless stabilising network would cost nothing.

What the display shows (1 LNA Noise Match.cdd):

  • Noise figure of the LNA: NF from 50 Ω against NFmin. They touch at 2.45 GHz, where the marker reads 0.618 dB.
  • Noise and gain circles at 2.45 GHz: the Noise and Gain Circles chart from the toolbar. Sopt sits at the centre. The three noise circles are 0.25, 0.5 and 1 dB above NFmin, and the three available-gain circles are 1, 2 and 3 dB below the maximum. The source stability circle is off the chart, because the stage is unconditionally stable.
  • Which part sets the noise at 2.45 GHz: NFpct as bars, one per part.
  • Gain and stability: dB(S21) and Rollett's K.

2 Receiver Budget

The receiver as system blocks, at 2.45 GHz and 290 K:

Block Part Gain NF
LNA Amp, Gain=G_lna NF=NF_lna G_lna, swept 4 to 24 dB 0.62 dB, bench 1's
RF filter Filter, 3rd-order Butterworth, 2.35 to 2.55 GHz −2 dB 2 dB, its loss
Mixer stand-in Atten, 7 dB −7 dB 7 dB, its loss
IF amplifier Amp, Gain=20 NF=4 20 dB 4 dB

An S-parameter run has no LO, so a mixer cannot convert in it. The stand-in is a 7 dB pad, the loss and the noise figure of a passive mixer. Bench 3 has a real one, and bench 6 runs the chain with a Mixer block and an LO.

MEAS1 writes Friis' formula for four matched stages as NF_friis:

F = F1 + (Lf − 1)/G1 + (Lm − 1)·Lf/G1 + (F4 − 1)·Lf·Lm/G1

The run's NF agrees with it to 1e-9 dB at every LNA gain.

LNA gain NF LNA's share IF amplifier's share
16 dB 2.12 dB 24.4 % 47.9 %
24 dB 0.90 dB 67.0 % 20.9 %

At 16 dB of LNA gain, the IF amplifier sets half the receiver's noise and the LNA a quarter. The receiver's gain there is 27 dB. The LNA dominates only above about 20 dB of gain.

What the display shows (2 Receiver Budget.cdd):

  • Receiver noise figure against the LNA's gain: the run's NF as dots on NF_friis. The marker is at 16 dB.
  • Each stage's share of F − 1: NFpct for each block against the LNA's gain. The LNA's share climbs and crosses the IF amplifier's.
  • Receiver gain.

3 Mixer Noise

A single Schottky-like diode (Is = 5 nA, N = 1.05, Rs = 4 Ω) to ground. The RF port reaches it through a series resonator at 2.45 GHz, and the LO through one at 2.1 GHz. The IF, 350 MHz, comes out through a low-pass to port 2. The LO is a P1Tone, PLO: an available power Plo behind 50 Ω.

HB1 has Noise ticked with NoiseSideband=1: the signal comes in at f_IF + f_LO = 2.45 GHz. The image is at f_LO − f_IF = 1.75 GHz. SW1 sweeps the LO's available power Plo from −10 to +14 dBm.

LO SSB NF DSB NF Conversion gain
0 dBm 7.96 dB 6.75 dB −13.40 dB
+6 dBm 4.73 dB 3.46 dB −9.20 dB
+10 dBm 4.38 dB 3.09 dB −8.49 dB

With too little LO the diode never switches, and the mixer does not convert: at −10 dBm the SSB noise figure is above 39 dB.

SSB is 1.27 dB above DSB at +6 dBm, not 3 dB. SSB − DSB is 10·log10(1 + image gain / signal gain), and the RF resonator reflects part of the image, so the image converts at about a third of the signal's gain.

At +6 dBm, the noise arrives through these input sidebands (NFsb):

Sideband Share
+1, the signal 38.7 %
−1, the image 17.2 %

The diode's own noise is the remainder, and it cannot be split by sideband. By part (NFpct), the diode is 53.7 %, port 1's termination at its other sidebands (mostly the image) 27.7 %, and the two resonators' losses 9.4 % (LR) and 9.2 % (LL). The diode sets half this mixer's noise: it is noisy only while it conducts, and the LO switches it on for part of every cycle.

The P1Tone is noiseless, as every source is in a harmonic-balance noise analysis: its 50 Ω is the LO's termination, not a noise source. A real LO's own noise reaches the diode through the LO resonator, which passes 1.75 GHz and 2.45 GHz almost as well as 2.1 GHz. To see what it costs, replace PLO by a V_1Tone behind a 50 Ω resistor: the resistor is thermal, and its share of the noise is the LO port's.

What the display shows (3 Mixer Noise.cdd):

  • Mixer noise figure against LO drive: SSB and DSB. The marker is at +6 dBm.
  • Conversion gain, RF to IF.
  • Where the noise comes in: NFsb as bars over the input sideband at +6 dBm.
  • Which part sets the noise: NFpct as bars at +6 dBm.

4 LNA Under a Blocker

Bench 1's LNA, part for part, with port 1 a P1Tone putting a 2.3 GHz blocker of Pblk into it. HB1 is a harmonic balance at the blocker, with Noise ticked and NoiseSideband=0, reading the noise figure at 2.45 GHz. SW1 sweeps Pblk from −40 to +10 dBm.

Blocker NF at 2.45 GHz
−40 dBm 0.618 dB, bench 1's S-parameter NF to 1e-4 dB
−2.5 dBm 0.67 dB
+2.5 dBm 1.62 dB
+10 dBm 4.45 dB

The blocker's own gain is 11.83 dB while it is small, and it is 1 dB compressed between +2.5 and +5 dBm. The noise figure has risen by 1 dB by then. A blocker 10 dB below the LNA's compression point costs almost nothing. A blocker at compression costs as much as the LNA's whole noise figure. The signal's gain at 2.45 GHz falls faster than the blocker's own, which is desensitisation.

What the display shows (4 LNA Under a Blocker.cdd):

  • LNA noise figure against a 2.3 GHz blocker. The marker is on the small-blocker end, bench 1's 0.618 dB.
  • Gain, blocker and signal: the blocker's own gain (Gblk_dB) and the signal's at 2.45 GHz (Gc).

5 Noise Sourcepull

Bench 1's transistor stage without its input match: Rg, the FET, Rs, LS, Rd and Rstab. A source tuner TS replaces the match and a load tuner TL holds 50 Ω. Each tuner carries its bias. LP1 is a sourcepull with Noise ticked: at every point of source_grid.gam (|Γ| = 0 to 0.9 in steps of 0.15, at 24 angles), it drives the stage from −30 to 0 dBm and reads NF at every step.

At −30 dBm
Lowest NF 0.39 dB, at Γs = 0.75∠15°
Highest gain 11.50 dB, at Γs = 0.60∠15°
NF from 50 Ω 0.97 dB

At 0 dBm of drive, the lowest NF on the grid has risen to 0.53 dB.

Source degeneration (LS) put the noise and gain optima on the same angle, close together. The lowest NF is 0.23 dB below bench 1's 0.618 dB. Most of that difference is the match's own loss, L1's 28.7 % share in bench 1, so a lower-loss inductor wins most of it back.

What the display shows (5 Noise Sourcepull.cdd):

  • NF and gain on the source plane, −30 dBm: NF contours from 0.5 dB in 0.25 dB steps, and gain contours from 7 dB in 1 dB steps.
  • NF on the source plane, −30 and 0 dBm: the same NF contours at both drive levels. The drive shrinks the low-NF region.

6 Receiver EVM

Bench 2's chain in an envelope run, with a real mixer:

  • SRC: a MOD_Source sending an NR carrier, 5 MHz, 15 kHz subcarriers, 64QAM, at 2.45 GHz. Noisy is ticked at 290 K, so its 50 Ω adds the input noise a noise figure is referred to.
  • LNA: Gain=16 dB NF=0.62 IP3=5 dBm, so it compresses.
  • FLT: the RF filter.
  • MIX: a Mixer block, −7 dB, NF=7.
  • VCO: the LO, a P1Tone at 2101.2 MHz and 7 dBm. Its PhaseNoise is bench 7's VCO at 3.5 V, copied from that bench's phase-noise trace with Copy as Phase-Noise Mask. The envelope run gives it a fundamental of its own, because it is far from the signal's frequency.
  • FIF: an IF filter at 349 MHz.
  • IFA: the IF amplifier, 20 dB, NF=4.

The IProbe Iif is after the IF amplifier. Each carrier is measured where it arrives, its power, gain, ACLR and EVM alike. Here that is the IF, 348.8 MHz (2.45 GHz − 2101.2 MHz). At −50 dBm the IF power is −24.0 dBm: the chain's 26.0 dB of gain (16 − 2 − 7 − 1 + 20). At −20 dBm the LNA has compressed it to 23.8 dB.

ENV1 has Noise and EvmSplit ticked: each point runs twice, the second time with every noise source and the phase-noise mask off. The EVM then splits into its noise part and its distortion part. SW1 sweeps the input power Pin from −95 to −20 dBm.

Pin EVM Noise part Distortion part SNR
−95 dBm 31.08 % 31.08 % 0.40 % 10.1 dB
−80 dBm 5.66 % 5.65 % 0.40 % 25.0 dB
−65 dBm 1.64 % 1.60 % 0.40 % 36.0 dB
−50 dBm 1.34 % 1.28 % 0.40 % 37.9 dB
−35 dBm 1.53 % 1.28 % 0.83 % 37.9 dB
−20 dBm 11.76 % 1.29 % 11.69 % 37.8 dB

There are three regimes:

  • Below about −70 dBm, thermal noise. EVM falls 1 dB for every dB of signal.
  • From about −60 to −35 dBm, the LO's phase noise. The SNR stops at 37.9 dB, and stays there however strong the signal is: the LO's phase noise multiplies the signal, so it grows with it.
  • Above −35 dBm, distortion. The LNA compresses.

The distortion part's floor, 0.40 %, is the NR signal's own EVM from its shaping, plus the filters' in-band ripple.

What the display shows (6 Receiver EVM.cdd):

  • EVM against input power: EVM, its noise part and its distortion part, in dB. The marker is at −50 dBm.
  • Signal over the noise the run added: the in-channel SNR, rising and then flat at the phase-noise floor.
  • Three constellations. At −50 dBm the points are tight. At −20 dBm compression scatters them. At −95 dBm thermal noise blurs every point into a cloud.

7 VCO Phase Noise

A common-base Colpitts on the built-in BJT (Kf = 1e-10, so its base current has flicker noise), with a 3.2 nH tank. A reverse-biased diode DV, coupled through 1 pF, tunes it from Vtune through 10 kΩ. OSC1 is an oscillator analysis with Phase noise ticked, from 100 Hz to 10 MHz off the carrier. SW1 sweeps Vtune from 3 to 9 V.

Vtune Fosc Kvco PN at 100 kHz Flicker corner
3 V 2092.7 MHz 17.1 MHz/V −106.6 dBc/Hz 6.9 kHz
3.5 V 2101.2 MHz 16.1 MHz/V −106.5 dBc/Hz under 10 Hz
4 V 2108.7 MHz 14.3 MHz/V −106.4 dBc/Hz 3.2 kHz
9 V 2155.6 MHz 6.6 MHz/V −105.6 dBc/Hz 42.3 kHz

At 3.5 V the phase noise is −128.8 dBc/Hz at 1 MHz, 20 dB per decade, which is white noise turned into phase noise. PNpct names the transistor (about 80 %), the tuning resistor Rt and the tank's load RL. Rt matters because its thermal noise is a voltage on the varactor, and the varactor turns it straight into frequency.

The flicker corner, below which phase noise falls at 30 dB per decade, moves with tuning. At 3.5 V it almost vanishes, because there the oscillation's frequency is insensitive to the transistor's flicker current. That makes 3.5 V the quietest place to run this VCO close in, which is why bench 6's LO is this curve.

Copying it: in the display, right-click the 3.5 V PN trace's legend entry and choose Copy as Phase-Noise Mask. The clipboard then holds PhaseNoise="100Hz:-49.20, …", which is what VCO's PhaseNoise in bench 6 holds.

What the display shows (7 VCO Phase Noise.cdd):

  • Tuning curve and Tuning sensitivity: Fosc in MHz and Kvco = d(Fosc)/d(Vtune).
  • Phase noise at 3.5, 6 and 9 V on a log offset axis. The marker is on 3.5 V at 100 kHz. The 6 V and 9 V curves bend up below their flicker corners. The 3.5 V curve does not.
  • Flicker corner against tuning, with its null at 3.5 V.
  • Which part sets the phase noise, 3.5 V: PNpct as bars at 100 kHz.
