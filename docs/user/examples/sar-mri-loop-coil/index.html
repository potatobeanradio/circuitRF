SAR: MRI Loop Coil

A 7 T surface coil beside a head phantom with every capacitor gap a port: openEMS runs once and keeps each port's fields, and a schematic then tunes, matches and optimizes the coil against S11, B1 and psSAR goals while B1 and SAR follow every value with no new EM run.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ SAR: MRI Loop Coil copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

A 7 T (298 MHz) surface coil beside a head phantom, designed the way coil designers work: every capacitor gap is a port. openEMS runs once, keeps each port's fields, and from then on the schematic tunes, matches and optimizes the coil while B1 and SAR follow every value with no new EM run. circuitRF's SAR is for research and engineering use; it is not a compliance tool.

The coil and the phantom are openEMS's own MRI_Loop_Coil tutorial, rebuilt from 3D-view objects. circuitRF reproduces that tutorial's numbers on the tutorial's own grid; this example runs on circuitRF's own, coarser grid, so it solves in about a minute and a half (see What was traded for speed, below).

The two cells

Loop Coil (its 3D view). An 80 mm (z) by 60 mm (y) loop of 5 mm strips at x = −130 mm, drawn as eight PEC sheets, with four gaps a third of a strip wide. Each gap is a 50 Ω lumped port: the feed at the bottom is port 1, the left, right and top gaps are ports 2, 3 and 4. Beside it is the head phantom: three nested ellipsoids (skin, skull, brain; semi-axes 100, 80 and 100 mm, shells 9 mm), solved as 2.5 mm voxels, in the tissue values openEMS's tutorial uses at 298 MHz (each with its density, which SAR needs).

Its one setup, CoSim: openEMS, 98–498 MHz in 41 points, first-order absorbing (Mur) faces, an end criterion of −40 dB, and a Sar group at 298 MHz — 10 g averaged SAR by cubes, B1 with B0 along +z, and Keep per-port fields on. Two field plots are saved: B1+ and SAR on the axial plane, both drawn from the schematic's fields analysis F1.

Coil Bench (a test bench). The coil's saved run as one EM Field Data block, EM1, on results/Loop Coil CoSim.openems.cemr, with its four pins named after the run's ports, and around it:

Part What it is
Cleft, Cright, Ctop the three gap capacitors, all set by the variable Cgap
Cf the capacitor across the feed gap, in series with the loop like the other three
Cm the series match from the 50 Ω source to the feed
P1 the 50 Ω source

and two analyses: SP1, S-parameters over 280–316 MHz, and F1, a fields analysis at 298 MHz that recombines the four ports' fields with the waves this circuit delivers into them. F1 is normalised per watt available from the source (norm=available), so a poor match shows as weak B1, as it would on the scanner.

Run it

  1. Simulate the Loop Coil's 3D view (setup CoSim). It needs openEMS; the first run offers to install it. Four openEMS runs, one per port, about 95 s in all, and a result record and a field set of 87 MB written beside the .s4p in results/.
  2. Simulate the Coil Bench. EM1 reads the saved result, so editing the setup later changes nothing it uses until the setup runs again; its Run settings… shows what differs. SP1 gives S11 and F1 gives B1 and SAR. The Messages pane's Open in 3D view shows them on the coil.
  3. Optimize. The Optimizer panel already holds the variables, the goals and an algorithm (simplex, seed 1). Press Run.

As drawn, with the tutorial's 5.4 pF in the gaps, a 33 pF feed capacitor and a 4.7 pF match, the coil is far off resonance on this grid: 0.3 % of the available power is accepted and the mean |B1+| in the brain is 0.010 µT per √W. The optimizer meets all three goals in 21 iterations and 40 simulations, about 4 s:

As drawn Best
Cgap 5.4 pF 7.450 pF
Cf.C 33 pF 14.10 pF
Cm.C 4.7 pF 5.061 pF
Match: S11 at 298 MHz, ≤ −20 dB −0.01 dB −20.5 dB
B1: mean |B1+| over the brain, ≥ 0.2 µT/√W 0.010 0.203
SAR10g: psSAR10g, ≤ 2 W/kg per W 0.005 1.80

No EM run starts during any of it. Each of the 40 simulations solves the circuit, recombines the stored fields and, for SAR10g, averages the recombined SAR over 10 g cubes from scratch. The run's notes name that goal as the expensive one.

The three gap capacitors and the two match capacitors are also on the Tuning panel. Drag Cgap with the 3D view's B1+ plot open and the plot follows the slider; the averaged SAR is recomputed when you let go.

The 10 g peak sits in the skin, level with the coil's side, at (−80.6, +33.1, −11.6) mm; on the tutorial's grid it is the mirror-image point at y = −29.2 mm. The loop is nearly symmetric about y = 0, so the two sides' peaks are close, and which side wins moves with the grid.

What was traded for speed

  • The grid. circuitRF places its own lines (CellsPerWavelength 30 at the sweep's 498 MHz) instead of the tutorial's 3.04 M-cell grid, a third of the cells. B1 and the 10 g average are within 3 %. The single-cell peak of local SAR is not: it is set by the few cells at the skin nearest the strips, and it reads 26 % low here. Read local SAR's peak from a finer grid, or from the averaged values.
  • The coil's tuning moved. The cells normal to the coil's plane are coarse on this grid, which lowers the loop's inductance (docs/design/sar.md §10.7). With the tutorial's own 2.5 Ω feed this grid resonates the loop at about 6.4 pF, not 5.4 pF. The circuit absorbs that: the optimizer finds the values for this grid, which is the point of keeping the capacitors out of the EM run.
  • The sweep. 98–498 MHz in 41 points (10 MHz apart), where the tutorial's runs 0.4–595.6 MHz in 497 points. With every gap a port the block has no resonance of its own, so 10 MHz is enough between points, and 298 MHz is one of them: the match goal and F1 read the block where it was solved.

To run on the tutorial's own lines, OpenEms.GridLines in the setup takes a grid from a file (the EM Setup reference, "Running on given grid lines").

Things to try

  • Change the phantom's tissues to circuitRF's own frequency-dependent ones (the technology editor's Add Tissue Materials, then Muscle, Bone (cortical) and Brain (grey matter); Skin (wet) states no density, so SAR refuses it). Re-run the 3D view: the fields belong to the tissues they were solved with.
  • See how uneven a surface coil's field is: measure Flat = B1pCoV(F1, "brain"), the spread of |B1+| over the brain divided by its mean, reads about 1.07 at the optimum.
  • Put a tolerance on the gap capacitors (dist=gauss sd=2% on Cgap's tuning row), add a yield spec (goal SAR10gY = psSAR(F1, 10) analysis=F1 le 2 use=yield) and run a yield analysis: every trial recombines the stored fields, and none starts openEMS.
