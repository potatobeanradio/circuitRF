LEO Satellite Downlink

A 20 GHz 16APSK single carrier through a transponder and a 600 km low-orbit pass at 30° elevation: P.618 rain, clouds and scintillation at 99.5 %, P.676 gas, ITU-R P.681 land mobile-satellite states, the pass's 400 kHz Doppler acquired and removed, and EVM, bit errors and outage over 40 drops.

A low-orbit satellite at 600 km sends a 16APSK single carrier at 20 GHz to a vehicle in a suburb, seen at 30° elevation as it sets. One schematic, Downlink, holds the transponder, the path and the terminal, and one envelope run over 40 drops reads the signal at the far end, drop by drop.

Part What it is
UPLINK The signal as it reaches the transponder: 16APSK at 10 Msym/s, root-raised-cosine roll-off 0.2, a 64-symbol preamble and 36 pilot symbols every 1,440 (Carrier[1]="SC;…"), at −3 dBm
IMUX, TWTA, OMUX The transponder: a 20 MHz input filter, a travelling-wave tube amplifier as a 40 dB gain block whose output intercept is 48 dBm, and a 30 MHz output filter. It sends 36 dBm, and its compression is the 2.6 % EVM at Itx
CH1 PathLoss=satellite Altitude=600 Elevation=30 Pass=setting: the slant range, its latency and the pass's Doppler. ITU-R P.618 rain, clouds and scintillation, and P.676 gas, at Availability=99.5. ITU-R P.681's land mobile-satellite states for a suburb (States=p681 StateEnvironment=suburban) at Velocity=10 m/s. The satellite antenna is 36 dBi, the terminal's flat panel 30 dBi
LNA, TERM The terminal: 30 dB, 1.5 dB NF

ENV1 is 0.2 ms of the carrier with Noise=yes and Equalizer=pilot: the preamble and pilot blocks train the receiver's equalizer, the frequency offset is measured and removed, and every symbol is decided. PASS sweeps it over drops 1 to 40.

The path

Quantity Value
Slant range, latency (link:CH1:Delay0_s) 1,075 km, 3.59 ms
Free space 179.10 dB
P.676 gas on the slant path 0.50 dB
P.618 rain, clouds and scintillation exceeded 0.5 % of the time 3.27 dB
Doppler Doppler_Hz, and its drift Drift_Hzps −399.28 kHz, −991 Hz/s

The carrier arrives 399 kHz low. The receiver acquires it from the power spectrum, fits it from the symbols' phases, and CFO_Hz reads it within 160 Hz in every drop with 10 dB of MER or more. The drift moves the frequency by a fifth of a hertz over the record, so it is read from the channel (Drift_Hzps), not from the signal.

Drops: the P.681 states

Each drop is in one state, because 0.2 ms at 10 m/s is 2 mm of road: GOOD (line of sight, light shadowing) or BAD (heavy shadowing and blockage). State is 1 for GOOD.

Measure Value
GoodFraction, the drops in the GOOD state 55 %
GOOD drops: MER, EVM 19.5 to 22.3 dB, 7.7 to 10.6 %, no bit errors
BAD drops from 21.7 dB, as clear as a GOOD drop, down to fully blocked (MER below 0 dB, BER 0.34 to 0.5)
Outage, the drops whose MER is below 15 dB (MerReq) 25 %, its exact 95 % interval 12.7 to 41.2 %

BER counts each drop's 6,132 bits; BERest estimates the same from the symbols' SINR and reaches below what one record can count: 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁴ in the GOOD drops. Here 16APSK reaches a bit error rate near 10⁻³ at about 16 dB of MER and passes 10⁻² below 15 dB, so the outage is the fraction of the time the vehicle is shadowed too deeply to decode before coding. In the GOOD state the margin is set by the noise, about 21 dB, and not by the transponder, whose 2.6 % EVM alone would read 32 dB.

What was traded for run time

The TWTA is the System amplifier: its soft limiter compresses (AM/AM) but does not turn the phase with drive (no AM/PM), which a tube does; its 2.6 % EVM is the limiter's alone.

40 drops take about 20 s in a Release build. The outage's interval is wide at 40: 400 drops take three minutes and narrow it to a few points. Noise=yes keeps the run from replaying the transponder's waves between drops, so each drop solves it again.
