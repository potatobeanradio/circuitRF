Envelope

The 48 V GaN power amplifier from the Harmonic Balance example, driven by a modulated signal — one NR 20 MHz carrier (30 kHz subcarriers, 64QAM, unclipped) — and simulated with the Envelope (modulated) analysis. The FET is the same reusable sub-cell.

The source: SRC1, a MOD_Source

A MOD_Source plays a modulated signal around Fc at an average power Pavg, behind Z. Each Carrier[i] is one line of text describing one carrier — standard, bandwidth, subcarrier spacing, modulation, offset from Fc, relative power and random seed:

NR;BW=20MHz;SCS=30kHz;Mod=64QAM;Off=0;P=0;Seed=1

Select it and press Design signal… in the inspector to edit the carriers in the Signal Designer: presets (LTE 20, NR 20, NR 100, two carriers at any spacing, four contiguous LTE), the spectrum and CCDF of the signal, and its PAPR and ideal ACLR. The signal is generated the first time it is needed and cached, so a sweep never generates it twice. Fc = RFfreq and Pavg = Pin are expressions, so the variables block drives them and a sweep of Pin sweeps the drive.

NR20_ACPR

One envelope run: 200 µs of the signal at Pin = 20 dBm average. The step is chosen from the signal's sample rate (Accuracy = standard is four steps per sample), and the record is split into time segments that run in parallel.

The run records a voltage at every labeled net (VDD, Vin, Vout) and a power at every IProbe: Iout in front of the output DC block and its 50 Ω load, Iin at the gate, IDC in the drain supply.

Cube At Shows
V, VSD each net the complex envelope at every harmonic of Fc against time, and the voltage spectrum (dBV/Hz)
PSD, ACLR each IProbe the delivered power spectrum (dBm/Hz); channel power and ACPR1/ACPR2, lower and upper (dBc)
Pavg_dBm, Gt_dB, DE_pct, NMSE_dB, PAPR_dB each IProbe at Iout: output power, gain, drain efficiency, error, PAPR
AMAM, AMPM (and _min, _max) each IProbe gain and phase against instantaneous input power
Gt_t_dB, Pin_inst_t_dBm each IProbe the memory cloud: every sample's gain and drive
Pin_avg_dBm, Pdc_W the run the available drive and the DC power

The MeasPA block adds three measure lines from the probe powers: Pin_del_dBm, the power delivered into the gate network; Gp_dB, the power gain from that to the load; and PAE_pct.

Simulate opens NR20_ACPR.cdd, the display laid out beside the bench: the power spectrum delivered at Iout and Iin with the voltage spectrum at Vout on the right axis, AM-AM (mean, minimum and maximum gain in each 0.5 dB input-power bin) and AM-PM at Iout, 2 µs of the envelope at the fundamental, the power, gain and efficiency table with the three measure lines, ACLR, and the memory cloud.

The figures at Iin are the power the gate network actually takes in. Its adjacent channels read blank: the distortion there flows from the device back toward the source, which is a power flowing the other way through the probe.

NR20_ACPR_vs_Pin

The same bench with the envelope inside a parametric sweep of Pin, 10 → 26 dBm in 4 dB steps: ACPR1 and ACPR2 against drive, the curve a linearity budget is read from. The five points run at the same time, each split into fewer time segments so they share the cores. NR20_ACPR_vs_Pin.cdd plots ACPR1/ACPR2 (both sides), output power and gain, drain efficiency and DC power against Pin, all at Iout, and the AM-AM of every drive level on one axis — they lie on one curve, because AM-AM against the instantaneous input power does not depend on the average drive. The table lists the MeasPA lines against Pin as well: a measure line stacks over a sweep with no edit. The memory plot draws one cloud per drive level.

Two carriers far apart

Change the source to two carriers (the Signal Designer's 2C NR 20 at spacing preset, e.g. 400 MHz) and run again. Carriers more than three channel bandwidths apart each get their own envelope fundamental, so the step stays at the single carrier's rate however wide the spacing is, and the IMD cube reports the carriers, IM3, IM5, IM2 and the second harmonics. Keep Max mix order at 7: at 5 the IM5 products are truncated by about a decibel.

To simulate the two carriers as ONE envelope instead — the whole signal in a single time vector around Fc, spanning both carriers and their IM3 — set Envelope to single on the analysis card. The step then follows the spacing rather than the channel: about 16× more steps at 400 MHz, so use fine. Carriers, ACPR, IM3L and IM2 agree with the default lattice run. Products far from Fc (IM5, and IM3 on the far side) depend on where the single envelope is centred, so read those from the lattice.

NR20_2C_400MHz_single: two NR 20 carriers 400 MHz apart on a narrowband test PA

This cell is a ready-made two-carrier case: NR 20 + NR 20 at ±200 MHz around 2 GHz, Pin 14 dBm, one envelope (Envelope=single). It uses a different amplifier from the cells above, TestPA_FET. The drain current is the same, and the cell adds the gate and drain charges, Cgd and Cds, and a 50 Ω gate shunt. The output is narrowband: a 20 nH drain line into a 5 nF bypass, then 20 nH + 0.3166 pF in series, resonant at 2.0 GHz, into 50 Ω. Its gain therefore falls away on both sides of 2 GHz, unevenly, and the two carriers come out about 5 dB apart at the load (C1 ≈ 18.7 dBm, C2 ≈ 23.5 dBm). A broadband PA would put them within a decibel. Vout is the load and Vd the drain. Iout sits in front of the load, so its power, ACLR, AM-AM and IMD are what reaches the 50 Ω.
