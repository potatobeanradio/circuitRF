TRL and De-embedding

TRL calibration from a Thru, a Reflect and a Line measured through two fixtures, the two fixture files it writes, a filter de-embedded with them and laid over the truth, and a GaN FET de-embedded to its intrinsic plane, where Cgs, Cgd, gm and Rds come out flat with frequency. The measurements are synthetic, made by circuitRF from cells you can open.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ TRL and De-embedding copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

Run the three numbered benches in order, top of the Workspace tree first:

  • 1 TRL Calibration solves the Thru, Reflect and Line for the two fixtures and writes them to fixtures/.
  • 2 Deembed DUT removes both fixtures from a measured filter and lays the result over the filter itself. It reads the files 1 TRL Calibration writes, so run that first.
  • 3 FET Deembed removes a GaN FET's extrinsic network, a 6-port, to show the device at its intrinsic plane.

Each opens its Data Display when it finishes. The Lab benches made the files in measurements/ and need not be run.

A part is rarely measured on its own. The instrument is calibrated at its connectors, and between those and the part sit fixtures: launches, lines, probe pads. TRL finds both fixtures from three measurements made through them, and a Deembed block then takes them off any measurement made the same way. The three benches above are the ones to duplicate for your own data.

What TRL is

The fixture between the instrument's port 1 and the part is fixture A, and the one between the part and port 2 is fixture B. Neither is known. TRL measures three standards through the same two fixtures:

  • Thru: A and B joined directly.
  • Reflect: A into a reflection, and the same reflection into B. Its value need not be known, only whether it is nearer an open or a short.
  • Line: A and B joined by a short extra length of uniform line. Its length and loss need not be known either.

From those three, both fixtures come out, and the part's own S-parameters follow from any measurement made through them. Very little has to be known about the standards, which is why TRL is used where good standards are hard to make: on a board, on a wafer, in a test fixture.

The model and the equations

Each two-port is written as a cascade matrix T, so that networks in a chain multiply. From S:

T = 1/S21 · [ −det S    S11 ]        det S = S11·S22 − S12·S21
            [ −S22       1  ]

and back again:

S11 = T12/T22    S12 = det T/T22    S21 = 1/T22    S22 = −T21/T22

With A and B the fixtures' cascade matrices, the measured Thru and Line are

Mt = A·B
Ml = A·L·B        L = diag(e^−γℓ, e^+γℓ)

where L is the extra length of line, with propagation constant γ and length ℓ. Then

Ml·Mt⁻¹ = A·L·A⁻¹

so the columns of A are the eigenvectors of Ml·Mt⁻¹, and its eigenvalues are e−γℓ and e+γℓ: the Line's electrical length and loss come out too. An eigenvector fixes a column only up to a scale, so one unknown is left over, and the Reflect fixes it: the same reflection seen through A and through B must be the same reflection. Its sign is the one choice the solve cannot make for itself, which is why reflectType says open or short. B follows from B = A⁻¹·Mt, and any measurement M gives the part:

DUT = A⁻¹ · M · B⁻¹

What the standards must be

  • Thru: the reference plane is its middle. The de-embedded part is the part between the two halves of the Thru.
  • Reflect: the same on both ports, highly reflecting, and known only as an open or a short. It need not be ideal; the one here is a short behind 1 mm of line and 0.5 Ω.
  • Line: the same line as the Thru's, longer by an electrical length between 20° and 160° at every frequency of the band. Near 0° or 180° the Line looks like the Thru and the two cannot be told apart. The Line's impedance is the impedance the part comes back referenced to.

The Line is best at 90° at the middle of the band. For a band f1 to f2, with fc = (f1 + f2)/2:

ℓ = c₀ / (4 · fc · √εeff)                    a quarter wave at fc
θ(f1) = 90° · f1/fc = 180° · f1/(f1 + f2)    ≥ 20° needs f2/f1 ≤ 8

So one Line covers an 8:1 band. For a wider one, use two Lines and two calibrations, each over its own band. The Line Standard cell is cut this way for 1 to 8 GHz, which puts its edges at exactly 20° and 160°: the 1 TRL Calibration display plots the Line's length against both limits.

The Deembed component

A Deembed is the opposite of an SnP. An SnP on a file adds that network to the circuit; a Deembed on the same file takes it away. It takes any even number of ports: ports 1 to N/2 are one side of the network, and the rest the other, in the file's order.

Wire it the way you would wire the SnP that adds the network, then swap the SnP for the Deembed and the part for its measurement. Fixture A's port 1 is the instrument side, so Port 1 — Deembed(A) — measurement. Fixture B's port 1 is the part's side, so measurement — Deembed(B) — Port 2. Neither needs flipping.

It works in every analysis an SnP works in: DC, S-parameters, harmonic balance, loadpull and sweeps. An SnP and a Deembed on one file, side by side, are a through connection to rounding, at the file's points and between them.

The inverse of a lossy network has gain, so a Deembed is active: a stability analysis that needs every part to be passive refuses it by name.

The FET

3 FET Deembed places the 8-finger GaN device from the Model Composer example (models/gan_4x125.cmodel) twice, biased as there: −0.8 V on the gate and 8 V on the drain. Its model carries an extrinsic network (pad capacitances, lead inductances, access resistances) between its outer gate, drain and source and its intrinsic device. GaN Extrinsic is that network as a cell, and Lab Extrinsic measures it as a 6-port: ports 1 to 3 the outer gate, drain and source, and 4 to 6 the inner ones.

On ports 1 and 2, a Deembed of that 6-port sits between the ports and the device, so the ports see the intrinsic device. The bias tees are outside the Deembed, so the DC current flows through it. On ports 3 and 4, the same device is measured as it is. Each copy's intrinsic elements are read from its Y-parameters:

Cgs = Im(Y11 + Y12)/ω      Cgd = −Im(Y12)/ω      gm = |Y21 − Y12|      Rds = 1/Re(Y22 + Y12)

The 3 FET Deembed display plots all four against frequency. After de-embedding they are flat, as an intrinsic device's are: Cgs within 0.4 % from 1 to 20 GHz, Rds within 0.01 %. As measured, the lead inductances and pad capacitances bend them: Cgs by 17 % and Rds by 57 % over the same band. That is the point of de-embedding to the intrinsic plane. Below 1 GHz both copies' Cgs dip: the 10 µH gate chokes are outside the Deembed, and at 0.5 GHz theirs is the admittance left in Im(Y11).

The source needs a net of its own. The Deembed's pin 3 (the outer source) goes to ground, and pin 6 (the inner source) goes to the device's source, which is on nothing else. Pins 3 and 6 are the two ends of one path through the network, the source lead. With both on ground, the current leaving one is not made equal to the current entering the other, so that path is never removed, and the answer is wrong while looking plausible. A run with both ends of a path on ground warns, naming the pins.

The HB analysis drives both copies at 10 dBm. IG1 and ID1 sit at the Deembed's pins 1 and 2, so Vgs_intrinsic, Ig_intrinsic, Vds_intrinsic and Id_intrinsic are the voltages and currents at the intrinsic plane.

The synthetic data

Every measurement here is made by circuitRF from cells whose values are stated, so the de-embedded answer can be checked against the truth, which a real measurement never allows. The files in measurements/ are inputs, and each Lab bench writes one. To make them again, run the bench and export S as Touchstone, or from a terminal:

circuitrf sparam "Lab Thru/schematic/Lab Thru.csch" -o measurements/thru.s2p
Cell What it is
Launch A Fixture A: 0.3 nH in series, 0.15 pF shunt, 9 mm of 40 Ω line. Port 1 is the instrument side
Launch B Fixture B: 12 mm of 60 Ω line, 0.12 pF shunt, 0.35 nH in series. Port 1 is the part's side
Line Standard 50 Ω line, a quarter wave at 4.5 GHz: 20° at 1 GHz and 160° at 8 GHz
DUT A five-section stepped-impedance low-pass filter, 20 Ω and 110 Ω sections, cut off near 5 GHz
GaN Extrinsic The FET's extrinsic network, its values the elaborated device's at 8 fingers
Lab Thru A then B: measurements/thru.s2p, 1 to 8 GHz, 141 points
Lab Reflect A into a short, and the same short into B: measurements/reflect.s2p
Lab Line A, the Line, then B: measurements/line.s2p
Lab DUT A, the filter, then B: measurements/dut.s2p
Lab Extrinsic The 6-port network from 0 to 20 GHz, so DC through the Deembed uses its real DC values: measurements/gan_extrinsic.s6p

The lines are lossy, with conductor and dielectric loss, and the two fixtures are deliberately different and mismatched. On this data, 2 Deembed DUT returns the filter to within 2e-10 in S, which is the precision the Touchstone files are written to.
