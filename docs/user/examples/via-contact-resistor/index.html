Via Contact Resistor

A thin-film resistor on a level of its own on a silicon back-end, contacted through vias, with a Metal1 line crossing under it that does not touch it.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ Via Contact Resistor copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

A thin-film resistor contacted through vias, on a silicon back-end. The technology, tech/rfic-Si_3LM_TFR_100um.ctech, is an example with generic round numbers, taken from no process.

The film has a level of its own between Metal1 and Metal2. The TFR in the schematic chooses Resistor, 50 Ω/sq, in its Film row, and the part draws a Metal2 head and a contact via at each end. Its length is the distance between the vias' inner edges.

What it shows

Metal can cross the film. The Metal1 line under the resistor joins the input through Via12 and passes under the film without touching it, and circuitrf lvs reads one resistor between two nets. On a level with metal, the same crossing would short that part of the film.

The film chooses the contact. Change Film to Resistor Hi, 500 Ω/sq, and the readout follows. It is the same level, so the artwork keeps its shape. A film on a level with metal would draw overlap contacts instead.

The substrate states a conductivity. Silicon, 10 kΩ·cm is σ 0.01 S/m with no loss tangent, so its loss falls as 1/f, as conduction loss does. Stackup ▸ A conducting substrate, in Help ▸ circuitRF Documentation, says when to state σ rather than tanδ.

Running it

ViaResistor/em/ViaResistor.cem is a planar run of all four levels at DC, with a coarse mesh and no edge mesh. It took 3 s and 3.8 GB on one machine, and reads 251.4 Ω against the part's 250 Ω: a via-contacted film reads long by up to half a via cell at each end.

A full-wave point on this artwork is refused on a 16 GB machine: the calibration standards alone need about 30 GB, at the coarsest mesh tried, because a 100 µm substrate asks for long standards and the stack's thin levels ask for small cells. Solve it in 3D on Palace if you need the sweep. EMerge cannot take this artwork, whose Metal1 and Metal2 are both closer to the film than the volume it solves a film as, and openEMS needs a cell the size of the thinnest gap, which costs hours.
