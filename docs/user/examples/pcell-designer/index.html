PCell Designer

Parameterised cells made in the PCell Designer and placed in benches: a tee whose stub ends in a drawn pad, placed twice with a drag handle on each pad, checked by LVS and exported as a PCell package; a 3-way Wilkinson divider whose isolation resistors meet on a drawn star pad; a spurline bandstop drawn as one polygon, its notch moved to 5 GHz by EM; a dual-mode square-patch bandpass placed by offsets; and a microstrip-slotline divider whose slot is cut in a drawn ground, solved in 3D.

Parameterised cells made in the PCell Designer from the parts in the Library, placed in benches, laid out, checked by LVS and simulated: a pad on a tee placed twice (§1-4), and four showcase cells, a 3-way Wilkinson divider (§5), a spurline bandstop (§6), a dual-mode patch bandpass (§7) and a slotline divider (§8). The PCell Designer reference page describes every control.

1. The designed cell

Open PadTee's schematic and choose Tools ▸ PCell Designer…. The canvas shows its layout at the values in the Value column. Type 600 um as Lpad's value and the pad grows; change Lstub and the pad moves with the stub's end. Nothing in the cell holds a coordinate: the lines are joined pin to pin, and the pad's far edge is tied to Lpad by Stretch by Parameter. The pad is Model="open", so in a circuit simulation it is an open end.

2. The bench

Open Bench and press Simulate: Bench.cdd shows |S21| and |S11|. Then Update Layout from Schematic draws the two placements, each at its own Lpad; select one in the layout and drag its Lpad handle. LVS compares the layout with the schematic and finds no error; its one warning is the ground pour under TL1, which no via stitches.

3. Export and import

Export… in the designer writes PadTee.cpcell. File ▸ Import ▸ PCell Package… in another workspace on the same board makes PadTee a cell there, placed exactly as here. From the command line:

circuitrf pcell check PadTee
circuitrf pcell render PadTee -o padtee.svg --set Lpad=600um
circuitrf pcell export PadTee -o PadTee.cpcell

4. Optimising by EM

With Create or update EM setup on, Update Layout from Schematic also writes the bench's Planar setup, and EM solves the two placed cells as copper. Set SP1 to run by EM and tune X1.Lpad. The pad is open in the circuit model, so Lpad moves only the EM response: em=correct, which searches on the corrected circuit model, stops at its start, because an open pad gives the circuit model no slope. em=direct (pattern search) moved it from 200 µm to 600 µm, its maximum, in 8 solves (9 min 19 s, coarse Planar).

5. A 3-way Wilkinson divider

Wilkinson3 splits 2.45 GHz three ways. Its input feeds a cross X1: the middle arm AM runs straight to the tee T2, and the outer arms fold once (B1, B3) and run beside it to T1 and T3. Each arm is a quarter wave of √3·Z0 = 86.6 Ω (Wa = 0.36 mm). Each tee's branch carries a 50 Ω resistor to the star pad SH1, a drawn pad whose Model="short" makes its three pins one node. A star of three resistors cannot be planar with all three outputs outside it, so R3 is a 1206 whose body bridges the middle arm.

Nothing is stretched, and the cell's two loops close by expression. The outer tees sit 6.2 mm off the axis and 6.2 mm short of T2, which is the fold. The lines to the resistors are lengths in Sp, and the pad's pins are written where R1, R2 and R3 end. A 0.3 mm line LQ2 keeps R2's pad off the bend BR2. Check finds no error at any parameter's minimum or maximum, and LVS finds none on the cell's own layout.

La = 19.5 mm was found in the designer's live response: it is a little over λg/4 (18.8 mm), because the cross and the tees take part of the junction. Simulate Wilkinson3Bench; Wilkinson3Bench.cdd shows the transmission and the input match, and the isolation. At 2.45 GHz:

−4.81 dB −4.95 dB −5.01 dB −32.1 dB −30.0 dB −47.4 dB −34.7 dB −45.0, −24.6, −25.4 dB

The three outputs are −4.77 dB plus the board's loss, within 0.2 dB of each other.

6. A spurline bandstop

Spurline is a 50 Ω line 16 mm long with an L-shaped slit. A slot g wide runs along the axis and turns out through the line's edge at its far end. That leaves a spur (W − g)/2 wide and Ls long, joined to the line at one end. The spur and the line beside it are a coupled pair, and the line stops where the spur is a quarter wave:

f0 ≈ c / (4 · Ls · √εav), εav = (εe + εo) / 2

The even- and odd-mode permittivities come from the coupled-line equations of Kirschning and Jansen (doi:10.1109/TMTT.1984.1132616). For two 0.45 mm strips 0.2 mm apart on this board, εe = 2.84 and εo = 2.41, so εav = 2.62. Ls = 8.5 mm gives f0 ≈ 5.45 GHz, and 5 GHz needs Ls ≈ 9.26 mm.

The slit's far end follows Ls by Stretch by Parameter, so Ls is a drag handle wherever the cell is placed. The cell is EM only. Simulate SpurlineBench (Planar, the setup in em/); SpurlineBench.cdd shows S21 and S11. The sweep runs 4.6-5.6 GHz in 25 MHz steps, because the notch is only about 50 MHz wide at −20 dB and a coarser sweep misses its bottom. On the setup's own planar mesh (41 points, about 2 minutes), the notch is at 5.38 GHz, with S21 at −33.9 dB at the nearest point.

The bench's Optimize runs em=direct with pattern search on X1.Ls, pushing S21 below −30 dB at 5 GHz:

  • The first pass (12 solves, 14 min) stopped at X1.Ls = 9.31 mm, which puts the notch at 4.93 GHz. Its steps were still a quarter of the range when the budget ran out, which is coarse for a notch this sharp.
  • A second pass with the range narrowed to 9-9.4 mm met the goal on its first new solve, at X1.Ls = 9.21 mm.

The fine sweep then puts the notch at 4.98 GHz, −33 dB at the nearest point. The closed-form estimate of 9.26 mm is within 0.05 mm of that.

7. A dual-mode patch bandpass

DualModePatch is a square patch a wide whose far corner is cut by d. Two 50 Ω feeds run along two adjacent edges, s from them. The feeds and the patch are joined by no net, so they are placed by offset: the patch from the first feed's open end, and the second feed from the patch's second pin. Each feed couples to one of the patch's two degenerate modes, and the corner cut on the diagonal between them splits the modes and couples them. The unperturbed patch resonates at

f10 ≈ c / (2 (a + 2ΔL) √εeff), εeff = (εr + 1)/2 + (εr − 1)/2 · (1 + 12h/a)^−½

where ΔL is the open edge's extension of Hammerstad (doi:10.1109/EUMA.1975.332206). With a = 15 mm, εeff = 3.45 and ΔL = 0.24 mm, which gives 5.22 GHz.

Simulate PatchBench (Planar); PatchBench.cdd shows S21 and S11. The sweep is 25 points over 4.9-5.5 GHz and takes about 3 minutes. It shows the two split modes either side of 5.22 GHz:

first pole second pole between them
Where S11 dips 5.08-5.10 GHz, −5.4 dB 5.35 GHz, −3.2 dB
S21 −7.3 dB −10.7 dB −11.7 dB at 5.25 GHz

The skirts fall to −17.5 dB at 4.9 GHz and −25 dB at 5.5 GHz.

The feeds are under-coupled, and no gap fixes it. The first design (s = 0.2 mm, d = 3.5 mm) peaked at −8.2 and −11.2 dB. em=direct (pattern search, 12 Planar solves, 25 min) on s and d asked for S21 above −3 dB over 5.1-5.3 GHz. It drove s to its minimum, 0.16 mm, which is the board's 0.15 mm copper-spacing rule, and d to 3.9 mm, the values the cell now ships with. That gained 1 dB and stopped there.

Why −3 dB is out of reach: at a single pole, S21 = 2Qu / (2Qu + Qe).

  • The −7.3 dB peak puts the external Q at about 2.6 times the patch's unloaded Q.
  • −3 dB needs about 0.8 times, so the coupling would have to grow by a factor of about 1.8.
  • An edge gap at the spacing rule cannot give that.
  • The patch's unloaded Q is low because an open patch on a 0.508 mm board is an antenna: much of its energy radiates.

A usable two-pole passband needs feeds that touch the patch, or an inset feed. Both are a different cell, since the patch and its feeds are then joined by a net.

8. A slotline divider

Slotline splits 5 GHz into two outputs 180° apart. A slot Ws wide is cut in the ground, and three microstrips on top cross it: the input at the slot's middle, and the two outputs Lsl either side of it. Each crossing is a microstrip-to-slotline transition:

  • the microstrip runs on Lms past the slot to an open end;
  • the slot runs on Lss past each output crossing to its shorted end.

Each is a quarter wave, so the open microstrip end becomes a short at the slot and the shorted slot end becomes an open at the microstrip. The slot carries the same voltage both ways from the input, so two outputs that cross it alike are in phase. Output 3 crosses it the other way round, with its port on the input's side and its stub on the other, which puts the two outputs 180° apart.

With the quasi-static estimate εs ≈ (εr + 1)/2 = 2.33 for a slot on this thin substrate, λs/4 at 5 GHz is c / (4 · 5 GHz · √2.33) ≈ 9.8 mm (Lss). The 50 Ω line's εeff = 2.86 gives λm/4 ≈ 8.9 mm (Lms).

The ground is one drawn shape on net 0, SH1. A polygon has no hole, so its outline runs in to the slot along a zero-width cut below the input crossing, around the slot, and back out the same way. The copper is then one piece with the slot inside it, and LVS reads its one pin on that piece. SH1 and both output lines are placed by offset from the input's crossing: the outputs at Dy = ±Lsl, and output 3 turned by 180°. The bench's ground pour stops at the cell's drawn ground, so the slot stays open. Planar cannot solve it, because Planar's ground is an unbroken plane. The bench's EM view is its 3D view (3d/SlotlineBench.c3d, made by New 3D View from Layout), which turns the drawn ground into copper with the slot cut in it, with an EMerge setup.

Simulate SlotlineBench; SlotlineBench.cdd shows the split and the two outputs' phases. The solve uses EMerge's Standard mesh (36,000 tetrahedra, 5.8 GB). It runs an adaptive sweep of 11 points over 4-6 GHz, fitted from 10 full solves, in 42 s:

The split is even to 0.05 dB and the outputs are 179-180° apart across the band. The loss is the board's, and it is mostly radiation:

  • At 5 GHz, |S11|² + |S21|² + |S31|² = 0.61, so 39 % of the input power does not come back out of the ports.
  • The dielectric (tanδ = 0.0037) and the copper along the 30-40 mm a wave travels account for about 0.3 dB, or 7 % of it.
  • The rest, about 32 %, leaves through the slot. A slot in a ground plane is a magnetic dipole, and on a 0.508 mm board with εr = 3.66 little of its field is held in the substrate, so it radiates into the open box on both sides of the board.

The outputs are also poorly matched (S22 ≈ −5 dB): a lossless three-port cannot be matched at every port, and this one has no isolation network. The transitions are centred above 5 GHz (S21 is still rising at 6 GHz), and longer Lss and Lms move the centre down.
