SAR: Phone Dipole

A system-check dipole 10 mm under a flat phantom of head tissue-simulating liquid, run at two bands by a band sweep, with psSAR1g and psSAR10g at the rated power beside published reference values.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ SAR: Phone Dipole copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

A phone-SAR system check: a half-wave dipole under a flat phantom of head tissue-simulating liquid, run at two bands with the band sweep, and psSAR1g and psSAR10g read at the rated power beside published reference values. circuitRF's SAR is for research and engineering use; it is not a compliance tool, and the table never says pass or fail.

The cell

System Check (its 3D view). The configuration a published regulator bulletin (FCC OET Bulletin 65 Supplement C, Edition 01-01) gives for a 1900 MHz system check:

  • a dipole 68.0 mm end to end, here two PEC strips 3.8 mm wide (armLo, armHi) on z = 0, with a 2 mm feed gap that is a 50 Ω lumped port;
  • a Flat Phantom above it: a 2 mm shell (circuitRF's own shell material, εr 3.7, lossless) holding 150 mm of head tissue-simulating liquid over 160 × 100 mm. Its floor is at z = 8 mm, so the liquid starts 10 mm from the dipole's axis, the bulletin's spacing.

The liquid is a table of target values, not one εr and σ: each band's run evaluates it at that band (εr 40.08, σ 1.371 S/m at 1.75 GHz; εr 40, σ 1.4 S/m at 1.95 GHz).

One setup, Template: openEMS, 1.7–2.1 GHz in 41 points, 40 mm of air on every side behind an 8-cell absorber, CellsPerWavelength 15, an end criterion of −40 dB, and a Sar group at 1.9 GHz with 1 g and 10 g cube-averaged SAR.

One band sweep, Bands: the Template at 1.75 GHz and 1.95 GHz, at 0.25 W incident on the feed.

Run it

Open the 3D view, then 3D ▸ Draw ▸ Phantom ▸ Band Sweep…. The dialog shows the sweep: its template, bands, power and reference values. Run makes one setup per band (Bands 1.75 GHz, Bands 1.95 GHz), each the Template at that one frequency with its sweep scaled to the band, and runs them in turn. About 2.5 minutes a band. Headless, the same run is

circuitrf em "System Check/3d/System Check.c3d" --bands Bands

The table

Band Accepted psSAR1g psSAR10g per W accepted (1 g / 10 g)
1.75 GHz 0.181 W 7.58 W/kg 3.90 W/kg 41.95 / 21.61 W/kg
1.95 GHz 0.248 W 9.98 W/kg 5.13 W/kg 40.19 / 20.66 W/kg

Both peaks are in the liquid's first cell above the feed, 10.6 mm up and within 1 mm of the dipole's centre. Every value is above the reference values the table shows beside it — 1.6 W/kg over 1 g (the national regulator's) and 2.0 W/kg over 10 g (the international guidelines') — and is meant to be: a system check puts a known power into a dipole a centimetre from the liquid to give the measurement system a large, repeatable number. It is not a device.

0.25 W incident, not accepted. The dipole is 68 mm, so it is matched near 1.94 GHz (S11 −22 dB there) and not at 1.75 GHz, where S11 is −5.6 dB and 28 % of the incident power is reflected. The table takes the reflected share off before the power reaches the dipole, which is why 1.75 GHz accepts 0.181 W of the 0.25. Choose accepted in the sweep to read the same SAR per watt delivered instead.

These are circuitRF's own results, not target values. A laboratory compares its measured psSAR1g with a target value for its dipole and phantom, from a standard that is not reproduced here. If you have target values of your own, enter them in the sweep's Targets and the table shows the difference.

The grid

Each band's grid is drawn from its own sweep, so the two runs are on different grids: 143 × 105 × 132 = 1.98 M cells at 1.75 GHz and 153 × 111 × 143 = 2.43 M at 1.95 GHz. CellsPerWavelength 15 was chosen from a convergence study of the same check at 1.9 GHz (docs/design/sar.md §14.5): against a grid of 20 cells per wavelength, 15 reads psSAR1g 1.6 % and psSAR10g 0.9 % high, and 10 reads 3–4 % low. Raise it in the Template and run the sweep again to see the difference; the band setups are made again from the Template every time.

Things to try

  • Place the dipole yourself. Select both arms, 3D ▸ Draw ▸ Phantom ▸ Place Against Phantom…, face liquid, separation 10 mm: the placement solves the move and the 3D view shows the dimension. Try 15 mm and run the sweep again.
  • A third band. Add 1.85 GHz to the sweep's bands. The Template is not changed; one more setup is made from it.
  • A phone instead of a dipole. Draw or import the device, place it against the phantom's liquid face or the Generic Head's ear spacer (3D ▸ Draw ▸ Phantom ▸ Generic Head (circuitRF's own)), set the sweep's power to the device's rated conducted power and its duty cycle if it transmits in bursts.
