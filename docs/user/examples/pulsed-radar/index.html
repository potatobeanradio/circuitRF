Pulsed Radar

A 48 V S-band GaN stage driven by 100 µs pulses at 10 % duty from a pulsed-CW source: pulse droop from channel heating and supply sag, pulse-to-pulse spread from drain lag, rise and fall, from one envelope run. The device is a synthetic radar-class GaN model with a three-pole thermal ladder.

A 48 V S-band GaN power stage driven by pulses: 100 µs of carrier every 1 ms from a pulsed-CW MOD_Source, simulated with the Envelope analysis. A pulsed-radar amplifier is specified by what happens inside a pulse — how far the output droops across the top as the channel heats and the drain supply sags, and how much pulses differ from one another (pulse-to-pulse stability). Harmonic balance cannot see either: it solves one steady state, so the device sits at one temperature and one trap state. The envelope run steps the device's thermal and trap states, and the supply's storage capacitor, as memory over time.

radar-gan: the device

The transistor is a synthetic GaN HEMT, meant to stand in for the kind of 48 V GaN-on-SiC part a pulsed S-band amplifier would use: a 12 mm device (N=48) carrying about 1 A/mm, with a three-pole thermal path from the channel to the flange (1.5 °C/W in all) and drain-lag trapping. No real device was measured: radar-gan/radar_gan_8x250.cmodel was built with Tools ▸ Model Composer… from simulated data.

PulsedPA: the stage under CW

The device at N=48 in a class-AB stage at 3 GHz, Vgs −2.6 V, Vdd 48 V, matched rather than put into 50 Ω: a low-pass L match on each side, to about 1.5 + j4.5 Ω at the gate and 4 + j4 Ω at the drain, the power optimum (5.5 + j6 Ω is the efficiency optimum, and dissipates half as much). A stage this size needs stabilising: 1 Ω in series and 10 Ω ∥ 50 pF in the gate, 5 Ω + 20 pF from the gate to ground, and 50 Ω in the gate feed. Without them it is unstable from 0.1 to 8 GHz. The drain is fed through a choke from a 470 µF storage capacitor, which the 48 V supply charges through 0.2 Ω: both are drawn on the schematic, so the supply sags during a pulse in the envelope run as it does on a real bench.

A harmonic-balance drive sweep, 20 … 34 dBm: Pout, gain, drain efficiency, PAE and the junction's rise (Tj_rise, read from the T pin, the net tj). This is the steady state of a pulse that never ends: the device at the temperature a CW drive heats it to. Under a second; every point converges. Past 34 dBm the solve fails to converge at most drives, so the sweep stops at the design drive.

PulsedPA_pCW: the stage on pulses

The same stage on a pulsed-CW source: 100 µs pulses every 1 ms (10 % duty), 0.5 µs edges, the first rising edge 10 µs into the record, and the source's Pavg the pulse-top power. Open the source's Design signal… to see the pulse.

One envelope run of 3 ms holds three whole pulse periods: about 105 s, as one segment. Before it starts, the Messages pane says what it will show: the slowest memory, the drain-lag state's 1 ms emission, needs about 5 ms to settle, so the run shows the first pulse from cold, not a settled train. Simulate opens PulsedPA_pCW.cdd: the output power over the run, the junction's temperature rise over the same time, the first pulse's top zoomed, and the per-pulse table. The output power is measured at Iout, an IProbe in series with the 50 Ω load, and the pulse figures are its rows; the run also records the voltage at every labeled net, tj among them.

  • The junction heats 47 K across the first pulse, from its 15 K quiescent rise to 63 K, and the storage capacitor sags 0.34 V (0.7 %). Under CW the same drive sits at 78 K.
  • Most of the heat arrives before the droop window opens. The channel's own 1 µs pole has heated it within the first few microseconds (the zoomed plot shows the output falling 0.26 dB over the first 10 µs of the top), and the window, 10–90 % of the top, sees the die's 20 µs pole and the start of the package's 0.5 ms one.
  • Pulse-to-pulse: 0.08 dB and 0.22°. After pulse 1 the junction cools to 4.8 K, below its 15 K quiescent value: the drain-lag state captured at the pulse's peak and emits over a millisecond, so the quiescent current is lower until it does. That is current collapse, and it is why pulse 1 differs from pulses 2 and 3.
  • Pin_avg_dBm, and Pavg_dBm and DE_pct (50.4 %) at Iout, are averages over the whole 3 ms run, off-time included. The run ends a whole period after the last pulse's rising edge, so the supply has recharged the storage capacitor that pulse drew on: stopped just after a pulse, DE_pct counts the capacitor's share of the pulse as free and reads high.

Settings traded away for speed

Setting Here Why, and what it costs
Accuracy draft (the envelope step is the signal's own rate, 32.6 ns) standard is 4× the steps. The pulse top is flat and the droop is thermal, so a coarser step costs nothing there; the edges are drawn with 15 steps each instead of 60
Edges 0.5 µs a real radar pulse's edges are often ~100 ns, which needs a 4× faster native rate and 4× the steps
Record three pulse periods, 3 ms a settled train needs about five of the 1 ms drain-lag emission and ten or more pulses

A sweep of the pulse width is not offered: a carrier is a quoted description, which a sweep variable does not reach into, so each width is its own run. Edit PW on the source and run again.

Not modelled here

Modulation inside the pulse (chirps, phase codes), pulsing the gate or drain bias with the RF, and radar range-sidelobe and spectral-mask figures.
