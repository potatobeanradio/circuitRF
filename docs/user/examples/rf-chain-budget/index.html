RF Chain Budget

A receive chain as a budget, stage by stage: a 2.45 GHz superhet, a four-element steered array into a combiner, a front end with a canceller and a dual-conversion receiver, each with its gain, NF, intercepts, compression, sensitivity and SFDR in closed form, and transferred to test benches whose S-parameter, harmonic-balance and envelope runs put a simulated number beside every one of them, the dual-conversion one conversion by conversion. And a 26 GHz link whose stages scale with the frequency: EIRP, the path, SNR, rate and DC power, swept over the carrier and both arrays.

Open a .cchain and the RF Chain Budget window shows its cascade. The benches beside it are its Transfer to Schematic: open one, Simulate, and its Data Display opens with the budget's number beside every simulated one.

Four receive chains at 2.45 GHz, in the same 4.5 MHz bandwidth, each already transferred:

  1. Superhet.cchain: an LNA, an RF bandpass filter, a mixer to a 350 MHz IF, an IF filter, an IF amplifier and an attenuator. Every stage is a System block with its own parameters.
  2. Array.cchain: four elements, each an LNA and a phase shifter, into a 4-way combiner steered 45° per element, then the superhet's receiver from the RF filter on. The combiner is the library's N-way Combiner; the phase shifter is composed of the library's own parts, an amplifier and a line.
  3. Front End.cchain: a canceller in front of an LNA, a filter and a second amplifier. The canceller subtracts a copy of a −40 dBm interference, 20 dB over the wanted signal, through a 10 dB coupler, with 0.02 dB, 0.2° and 0.05 ns of error in its reference path: 47 dB of depth.
  4. Dual Conversion.cchain: the superhet's front end to a 350 MHz first IF, then a roofing filter, an IF amplifier, an image filter and a second mixer to a 21.4 MHz second IF, an IF filter and an amplifier. Two mixers are more than one simulation run holds, so most of its benches are written once per conversion (below).

The superhet also has two blockers at its input, 150 MHz and 20 MHz above the signal, and asks for the demo bench. The dual-conversion receiver has one blocker on each mixer's image, and asks for the demo bench too.

And a link: 26 GHz Link.cchain, a 16-element transmitter 200 m from an 8-element receiver at 26 GHz, in NR band n258 (24.25 to 27.5 GHz), whose stages scale with the frequency. See The 26 GHz link.

The formulas, the parts and the benches are in Reference ▸ RF Chain Budget.

The array's NF is one element's, and its SNR is four elements' signal. Four in-phase signals add in amplitude and their uncorrelated noise in power, so the combiner gains 6.02 dB of signal and none of noise. The SNR rises by 10·log10 4 = 6.02 dB over one element's, and the MDS per element falls by as much. Its NF, 4.09 dB, is that of one element's branch, the combiner's loss and the receiver after it, by Friis' formula. It is higher than the superhet's because the phase shifter's 5 dB comes right after the LNA.

The two conversions make the whole chain by the same sums. F = F₁ + (F₂ − 1)/G₁ = 1.4116 + 2.6190/5.6234 = 1.8774, 2.74 dB; 1/IIP3 = 1/IIP3₁ + G₁/IIP3₂ = 4.0773 + 5.6234 × 0.8968 = 9.1207 mW⁻¹, −9.60 dBm. The second conversion starts at MIX1's output, −62.5 dBm at 350 MHz. Its Envelope and Demo sources are at 2302 K, the noise the first delivers, so the SNR they measure is the whole chain's at every node; its 1-Tone source is at 290 K, where a noise figure is stated.

Each mixer's image filter is right in front of it, as the budget takes it: the RF filter before MIX1, IMGBPF before MIX2, with the IF amplifier ahead of it. With the amplifier between the image filter and MIX2 instead, its own noise at MIX2's image reaches the mixer, and the second conversion's NF reads 1.4 dB over its budget. And a roofing filter, IF1BPF, comes before the IF amplifier: without it IF1A sees MIX1's sum product at 4.55 GHz, and its third-order term mixes that with the IF onto the IF's own IM3, which the budget does not have. The whole chain's IIP3 then reads 0.44 dB under the budget.

Try it. In the array's window set the combiner's N to 8: the array gain becomes 9.03 dB and the SNR rises 3 dB, while the NF does not move. Set its Topology to Tree and the block view draws three levels of 2:1; set Kind to Active and an amplifier follows each.

The benches

Each chain has these three, written by Transfer to Schematic, and a Levels bench (below); the array has two more:

  • 1-Tone: a P1Tone per element and a Port at the output. With the mixer, harmonic-balance noise with the LO running gives the SSB NF and the conversion gain; a drive sweep at the predicted IP1dB gives the compression point.
  • 2-Tone: a PnTone per element, two tones 1 MHz apart, 50 dB below the predicted IIP3. The intercepts, and each stage's gain and OIP3 at its output.
  • Envelope: a MOD_Source per element sending an NR 5 MHz carrier, its 50 Ω noisy at 290 K. The SNR in the carrier's 4.5 MHz and the EVM's noise part.

In front of the mixer each bench has an isolator, a circulator with its third port terminated: it terminates the mixer's image at 290 K, which is what its SSB noise figure is stated against. Delete it from Superhet 1-Tone and the NF reads 0.28 dB higher: the RF filter reflects the image instead of terminating it, and the mixer's own noise source then develops more noise there than across a matched termination.

In the array each element has a line in front of it, PATH2 to PATH4: the arriving wave's extra path, 45° per element. Each element's phase shifter is set to take it out again, so the four arrive at the combiner in phase.

  • Array Steering: a plane wave into the four elements — one Port into a Combiner used as a splitter, its path k delayed by the wave's extra path — steered by the phase shifters from the VAR block's theta_steer, and read at the combiner. A sweep of theta_in from −90° to 90° within a sweep of theta_steer over −30°, 0° and 30° draws the array pattern, and beside it the closed-form array factor. Set taper to 6 and the sidelobes fall; set d_over_lambda to 1 and grating lobes appear.
  • Array Combiners: the same four elements into each of the combiner's four variants, side by side, each from its own wave at broadside: gain and NF from one S-parameter run, beside each variant's budget.

400 MHz of bandwidth (an FR2 channel), two streams (two polarisations), a required SNR of 10 dB. Worked out by hand:
PA output 24.01 − 8 = 16.01 dBm
EIRP 16.01 − 0.71 + 5 + 20·log10 16 = 44.38 dBm
Free space 20·log10(4π · 200 m · 26 GHz / c) = 106.77 dB, and 0.09 dB/km × 0.2 km = 0.02 dB more
At each receive element 44.38 − 106.77 − 0.02 + 3 = −59.41 dBm, below the receiver's ceiling of −45.38 dBm

The mixer sets the receiver's compression (89 % of its 1/IP1dB): it comes after the combiner, where the eight elements' signals arrive in phase, and the Notes say so. The cascade table's NF left column says the stages after the LNA may have 16.7 dB of NF together before they cost the chain 1 dB.

The sweeps. The Link tab's Sweeps row asks for three, each with its own tab:

  • the carrier from 20 to 40 GHz, beyond n258 to show the trend: the rate falls from 9.9 to 7.2 Gb/s as the PA's output falls past its knee, the loss and the noise rise, and the free-space loss rises, since a fixed-gain element's aperture shrinks with the wavelength; and the DC power steps where the LNA needs a third stage;
  • the transmit array from 4 to 1024 elements: the rate climbs until the received power meets the receiver's ceiling, then stops;
  • the receive array from 1 (no combiner) to 64 elements, sizes that are not powers of 2 among them, which the Levels topology takes.

The benches. The link adds a 26 GHz Link TX bench: one transmit element's PA under an NR 64-QAM carrier, swept over its back-off. At the link's 8 dB the output is 0.92 dB below the budget's Psat − 8 dB, the EVM 6.3 % and the adjacent-channel ratio −30.3 dBc; at 12 dB, 0.41 dB, 2.9 % and −36.9 dBc. The receiver's own benches run at the −59.41 dBm the link delivers to each element: its 1-Tone bench reads −0.0001 dB in gain and +0.0003 dB in NF, its Steering and Combiners benches 10⁻¹⁴ dB, its 2-Tone bench +9×10⁻⁴ dB in IIP3 and +6×10⁻⁴ dB in OIP3. Its Levels bench drives two tones each at the link's −59.41 dBm, 24 dB under the receiver's input P1dB, and reads the signal within 0.0015 dB of the budget up to the filter and 0.013 dB under it at the mixer's output, the IM3 0.030 dB under: the tones' own slight compression, at the power the link really delivers. Its Envelope bench, eight NR carriers of 50 MHz each noisy at 290 K, reads the SNR in the carrier's 48.6 MHz 0.02 dB under the budget's 43.15 dB, and the output power 0.006 dB under. The benches' NR carriers use 15 kHz subcarriers, where an n258 channel would use 60 or 120 kHz; the spacing changes neither the budget nor what these memoryless blocks do to a carrier's SNR and EVM.

Levels, blockers, the canceller and the demo

  • <chain> Levels (every chain): two tones at the input power, the signal and its IM3 at every node. Its display puts them beside the budget's, with the noise floor and NF from the 1-Tone bench, each blocker from the Blockers bench, and the SNR and the margin to compression, all against the node (0 the input, k stage k's output).
  • Superhet Blockers: the blockers alone, an isolator after every stage so each node reads the wave going forward. The RF filter takes 10 dB off the one at 150 MHz; after the mixer it is at 500 MHz, and the IF filter takes 38 dB more.
  • Front End Canceller: the canceller with real signals. A wanted 64-QAM and QPSK pair under a three-carrier interference (10, 5 and 20 MHz), each from a MOD_Source the Signal Designer edits. Its display: the depth against the phase error, the amplitude error and the delay mismatch at each interference carrier, simulated beside the closed form; the wanted carriers' EVM and SNR, and their constellations, with and without cancellation. Set phase_err or delay_err in its VAR block and Simulate again.
  • Superhet Demo: the superhet demodulated end to end, NR 64-QAM and DBPSK, the LO with a phase-noise mask, the input power swept from noise-limited to compression-limited: the constellation, the EVM beside the budget's 1/√SNR, and its noise and distortion parts.

Two conversions

Dual Conversion is written as two chains: Dual Conversion IF1 …, the front end to MIX1's output at 350 MHz, and Dual Conversion IF2 …, from there to the output at 21.4 MHz. Each has its own 1-Tone, Levels, Blockers, Envelope and Demo bench, and each display sets the simulation beside that conversion's own budget. One harmonic-balance noise run takes two tones and one envelope run two fundamentals, and every LO is one, so no single run of these holds both mixers. The 2-Tone bench is one run of the whole chain: four tones at MaxMixOrder 5, the most the 600-product cap allows.

The second conversion's benches start at MIX1's output: their sources are at 350 MHz and −62.5 dBm, the level MIX1 delivers. The Envelope and Demo sources are also at 2302 K, the noise MIX1 delivers (the MOD_Source's Temp, 2028.93 °C), so the SNR they measure is the whole chain's, node by node; the 1-Tone bench's source is at 290 K, the temperature its NF is stated at.

Images. The budget takes each image as filtered; a blocker at an image's offset shows how well. Blocker 1, at −40 dBm, sits on MIX1's image, 700 MHz below the signal at 1.75 GHz. The RF filter takes 55.4 dB off it beyond its IL, and after MIX1 it is on the IF itself, at 350 MHz and then at 21.4 MHz: at the output 25.4 dB under the signal, which it was 30 dB over at the input. Blocker 2, at −50 dBm, sits 1 MHz inside MIX2's image band, 41.8 MHz below the signal. The RF filter and the roofing filter pass it, MIX1 moves it to 308.2 MHz, IMGBPF takes 47.0 dB off it, and MIX2 puts it at 20.4 MHz, inside the second IF's channel, 27.0 dB under the signal. Exactly on the image, it would land on the same frequency as blocker 1 after MIX2, which a harmonic-balance run cannot tell apart, and Transfer would leave that Blockers bench out and say so.

The gain, NF and intercepts agree to the drive levels the benches use. The compression point is the cascaded-P1dB formula's own approximation: two stages compressing at similar levels compress a little later together. The SNR is one noise record's: four seeds of the array read 29.17 to 29.42 dB against 29.37 dB.

The array's Steering pattern sits on the closed-form array factor to 3×10⁻⁹ dB within 3 dB of each peak, and its gain, NF and array gain at each peak, like every variant's gain and NF on the Combiners bench, to 10⁻¹⁴ dB: an S-parameter run of ideal blocks has no drive level to differ by.

The superhet's levels sit on the budget to 2×10⁻⁵ dB (signal) and 8×10⁻⁵ dB (IM3) at every node, its blockers 0.001 to 0.003 dB under it, the blockers' own slight compression. The canceller's depth is the closed form's to 1.4 % of the depth at every carrier and error, and cancelling takes the 64-QAM carrier's EVM from 840 % to 3.2 %. The demo's SNR is the budget's to 0.2 dB where noise dominates; above that the LO's phase noise holds the EVM at 1.2 % until compression.
