Capacitance Extraction

A pad beside a microstrip line, solved for its capacitance by Palace's electrostatic solver in about fifteen seconds: how to pick the terminals and the ground, how the Maxwell matrix becomes the capacitors a schematic wants, and each answer checked against a parallel-plate estimate and the line calculator.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ Capacitance Extraction copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

A square pad beside a 50 Ω microstrip line on a two-layer FR-4 board, solved for its capacitance by Palace's electrostatic solver in about fifteen seconds. Each answer is checked against a number you can work out by hand: the pad against a parallel-plate capacitor, and the line against the line calculator. The note AN-03 Extracting capacitance (Help ▸ circuitRF Documentation ▸ Application Notes) says when this is the right tool and when it is not. Test Pad Capacitance, the next example, uses the same method on a multilayer board with no nets.

First: Simulate will ask you to install Palace

Opening this workspace needs no solver. The first time you press Simulate on a setup without Palace, the run is refused with Install Palace …. That is the expected first step. Everything below can be looked at before anything is installed.

The board

Pad and Line/3d/Pad and Line.c3d is drawn in the 3D editor from seven variables (the Variables panel, the x button):

Variable Value What it is
h 0.508 mm FR-4, εr 4.4
t 0.035 mm the copper
w 0.92 mm the line's width: 50 Ω on this laminate, from the line calculator
len 20 mm the line's length, and the board's
brd 16 mm the board's width
a 2 mm the pad's side
gap 0.5 mm copper to copper, line to pad

The objects are ground (the bottom copper), substrate, line and pad. None of them carries a net.

The two walls at the line's ends are zero-charge walls (PMC in the air-box face menu). No field line ends on one, so the line carries on through it as if it were infinitely long. That is what lets the answer be divided by the length and read as a capacitance per metre, with no end effect in it. The other walls are 2 mm of air beyond the board and zero-charge too. The ground plane's own bottom face is the box's floor.

How the setups were made

Both setups are in Simulate ▸ Setup Analyses…. To make the first one yourself:

  1. Add, then choose Electrostatic and Palace.
  2. In the Terminals table press +, name the row pad, press its Pick button and click the pad in the 3D view. The row fills with the pad's piece of metal, and the pad is selected in the view.
  3. + again, name it line, Pick, click the line.
  4. Open the Ground list (▾). It lists every piece of metal not in a terminal. Choose ground.

The second setup, Pad, line floating, has the pad row only and the same ground. The line is in no terminal and is not ground, so it floats: Other metal is left at Float, its default.

Capacitance Extraction: the numbers

Measured once on an Apple M4 with 10 cores and 16 GB, macOS 27.0, Palace 0.18.1 on 10 processes and Gmsh 4.15.2, at the Standard preset. A time is the whole run's wall clock. A memory figure is Palace's own peak.

Pad and line took 14 s, with a peak of 2.6 GB. The result (results/Pad and Line Pad and line.palace_es.npy) holds two matrices:

C (Maxwell) C_mutual (circuit)
pad line pad line
pad 532 fF −31 fF 501 fF 31 fF
line −31 fF 2,460 fF 31 fF 2,429 fF

C is the Maxwell matrix: the diagonal is a terminal's capacitance with every other terminal grounded, and the off-diagonal entries are negative. C_mutual is what you would draw in a schematic: the diagonal is each terminal's capacitor to ground, the row sum of C, and the off-diagonal is the capacitor between the two. So the pad has 532 fF to everything, made of 501 fF to ground and 31 fF to the line.

Pad and Line.cdd, beside this file, shows both matrices side by side once Pad and line has run: the columns headed Maxwell C are C, and those headed Circuit C are C_mutual.

The pad against εA/d

A parallel-plate capacitor with the pad's area over the laminate gives ε₀·4.4·(2 mm)²/0.508 mm = 307 fF. The solve gives 532 fF with the line grounded. The parallel-plate formula therefore misses 42 % of the pad's capacitance. That share is the fringing field: from the pad's edges, from its top surface through the air, and to the line. On a small pad over a thick laminate, fringing is not a correction. It is nearly half the answer.

The line against the line calculator

The line's capacitance to ground is 2,429 fF over 20 mm, which is 121.5 pF/m. A lossless line's capacitance per metre is C′ = √εeff/(c·Z₀). The line calculator (circuitrf impedance --tech tech/two-layer-fr4.ctech --layer "Top Copper (1 oz)" --width 0.92mm, or the parameter dialog of an MLIN) gives Z₀ 49.95 Ω and εeff 3.327 in its circuit-model column. That is 121.8 pF/m, 0.3 % from the solve. Two different methods agree on a number every RF designer already trusts, so the setup is right.

The gap sweep

The coupling between pad and line falls quickly as the gap opens. Set gap in the Variables panel and press Simulate again; the 3D view reshapes and the run re-meshes.

gap pad to line, C_mutual
0.25 mm 57 fF
0.5 mm 31 fF
1 mm 13 fF

Each point took 14 s. A 3D setup has no sweep of a variable that changes the geometry, so the three points are three runs. Each run writes the same result file, so copy it aside if you want to keep one.

Floating versus ground

Pad, line floating gives the pad 531.48 fF, against 531.87 fF with the line grounded. A floating conductor carries no net charge, and removing it from the matrix is exact: C_pad − C_pl²/C_line = 531.87 − 31.00²/2,460.11 = 531.48 fF, the floating run's own number. Here it hardly matters, because the line takes only 31 fF of the pad's field. Where floating metal sits between a conductor and its ground, it matters a great deal: Test Pad Capacitance shows a test pad reading less than half its capacitance because its ground planes were left floating.

The field

The 3D view's Field Plots group holds E through the pad: |E| for the pad's solution, on a clip plane across the board through the pad's centre. Run Pad and line, tick the plot, and drag its position slider in the Properties Inspector to move the cut along the line. The field is strongest in the laminate under the pad and in the gap, and the fringing that the parallel-plate formula misses can be seen curling round the pad's edges.

What is deliberately not here

Results. Run the setups yourself; each takes about fifteen seconds. The answers depend on the solver version, and a committed result would go stale without anyone noticing.
