Symbolically Defined Device (SDD)

The FET is the one every harmonic-balance reference in circuitRF is checked against, at the Loadpull and Pursuit example's 1.25 mm periphery, 48 V and −3.05 V bias, 2 GHz and polar load grid (load_grid.gam), with more load points where the optima fall.

What an SDD is

A Symbolically Defined Device describes a part by the currents at its ports as expressions of the voltages across them. There is no compiled model behind it: what you write is the model.

  • Ports. An SDD with N ports takes 2N nets, a + and a − for each. The FET here is a 2-port, Vin 0 Vout 0: port 1 is gate to source (ground), port 2 drain to source. Inside the equations _v1 is Vgs and _v2 is Vds.
  • Equations. I[p,w] is a contribution to the current into port p's + net, out of its − net. Every contribution to one port is summed.
  • Weights. The second index, w, says how the expression becomes a current. circuitRF evaluates I[p,w] in the time domain, takes its spectrum, multiplies it by a weighting function H[w](f), and adds the result to port p's current:
    • w = 0, H[0] = 1: the expression is a current. A resistor, a diode, a transconductance.
    • w = 1, H[1] = j2πf: the expression is a charge, and the current is its time derivative, dQ/dt. A capacitor. At DC it passes nothing, as a capacitor should.
    • w ≥ 2: weights you define, as expressions of freq (H[2]=1/(1+j*2*pi*freq*tau) is a low-pass, for one).
  • Everything else on the line is a parameter the equations read (B, TV0, Cgs0 …). Edit one on the schematic and the model changes; sweep it, tune it or optimize it like any other value.

circuitRF differentiates every expression exactly (automatic differentiation), so the conductances ∂I/∂V and capacitances ∂Q/∂V the solver needs come from the equations you wrote, with nothing else to supply. Write charges, not capacitances: a charge Q(V) whose derivative is the capacitance you want conserves charge in harmonic balance; a capacitance written directly does not.

1 Drain Current

The least an FET model can be: the drain current as a function of both port voltages.

I[1,0] = _v1/50
I[2,0] = Periphery_mm*(B*TC*tanh(_v2*a*(tanh(g*(TV0-_v1+_v2*th+Sc*ln(exp(-(Sv-_v1)/Sc)+1)))+1))
         *ln(exp(-(2*TV0-2*_v1+2*_v2*th+2*Sc*ln(exp(-(Sv-_v1)/Sc)+1))/TC)+1)*(_v2*lam+1))/2

I[2,0] is the drain current Ids(Vgs, Vds): the soft pinch-off (Sv, Sc), the transconductance's shape (TV0, TC), the knee (tanh(_v2*a…)), output conductance (lam) and scale (B, times the periphery). I[1,0] = _v1/50 gives the gate 50 Ω to ground, which is how this reference model sets its input impedance. Nothing here stores charge, so the device is instantaneous: its loadpull optimum sits on the real axis.

2 Gate Current

The gate is a Schottky diode to the channel, and a high enough drain voltage breaks down to the gate. Both join the equations as further w = 0 currents:

Igs = Periphery_mm*Isg*(if(_v1/Vtg<40, exp(_v1/Vtg), exp(40)*(1+_v1/Vtg-40)) - 1)          forward gate conduction
Ibr = Periphery_mm*Ibk*if((_v2-_v1-Vbr)/Vbk<40, exp(…), exp(40)*(1+…-40))                  gate-drain breakdown past Vbr
I[1,0] = _v1/50 + Igs - Ibr
I[2,0] = Ids + Ibr

The breakdown current leaves the drain and enters the gate, so it appears in both ports with opposite signs: a current between two terminals of a multi-port SDD is always written that way. The if() continues each exponential along its tangent past an argument of 40. The model is the same where the device works, and a Newton iterate that overshoots can't overflow. Near compression the gate swings positive and conducts, which clamps the drive: maximum power falls by 0.2 dB.

3 Charges (1D)

Now the device stores charge. Two charges on the w = 1 path, each a function of one voltage:

I[1,1] = Periphery_mm*(Cgsp*_v1 + Cgs0/2*(_v1 + ln(cosh(Kgs*(_v1-Vgs0)))/Kgs))      Qgs(Vgs)
I[2,1] = Periphery_mm*(Cdsm*_v2 + (Cds0-Cdsm)*Vds0*(1-exp(-_v2/Vds0)))               Qds(Vds)

Differentiate each and you get the capacitance you meant: Cgs(Vgs) = Cgsp + Cgs0/2·(1 + tanh(Kgs·(Vgs − Vgs0))), rising from 0.3 to 1.3 pF/mm as the channel opens; Cds(Vds) = Cdsm + (Cds0 − Cdsm)·exp(−Vds/Vds0), falling from 0.5 to 0.2 pF/mm as the drain voltage rises. Each is a 1D nonlinear capacitor: its charge depends on its own voltage only. The output capacitance must be resonated by the load, so the optimum loads move up into the inductive half of the chart.

4 Charges (2D)

Real FET charges depend on both voltages. Two changes make them two-dimensional:

Qgs  = Periphery_mm*(Cgsp*_v1 + Cgs0/2*(_v1 + ln(cosh(Kgs*(_v1-Vgs0)))/Kgs)*(1 + Dgs*tanh(-_v2/Vgsd)))
Qgd  = Periphery_mm*(Cgdm*(_v1-_v2) - (Cgd0-Cgdm)*Vgd0*(1-exp(-(_v2-_v1)/Vgd0)))
I[1,1] = Qgs + Qgd
I[2,1] = Qds - Qgd
  • Qgs now also falls as Vds rises (the channel pinches off at the drain end). The gate charge depends on Vds, so ∂Qg/∂Vds is a transcapacitance: a change of drain voltage moves charge at the gate.
  • Qgd is the gate-drain charge, a function of the voltage between the two ports, Vgd = _v1 − _v2, with Cgd(Vdg) falling from 0.15 to 0.04 pF/mm as the drain rises above the gate. Like the breakdown current, a charge between two terminals is added at one port and subtracted at the other.

Cgd is feedback from output to input, and it costs gain: Gt at maximum power falls from 15.3 to 14.9 dB.

5 Cgd as an SDD Block

The same model with Qgd taken out of the FET and drawn as its own one-port SDD, CGD, connected from gate to drain:

SDD:CGD  Vin  Vout     I[1,1] = Periphery_mm*(Cgdm*_v1 - (Cgd0-Cgdm)*Vgd0*(1-exp(_v1/Vgd0)))

Its one port's + net is the gate and its − net the drain, so its _v1 is Vgd. That is a 1D nonlinear capacitor anywhere in a schematic, between any two nets, written in one line. The loadpull reads exactly what bench 4 reads. Equations inside one SDD and equations split across several are the same circuit, which is how a large model can be assembled from blocks you can check one at a time. (A NonlinearC component does the same job as a polynomial C(V); an SDD takes any expression.)

6 Bias Loop (control current)

An SDD equation can read the current in another part as _cn, bound by C[n]=<instance>. Here that builds an active bias controller: an SDD that senses the drain supply's current and sets the gate bias so the quiescent current stays at its target, whatever the transistor's threshold.

SDD:BIAS  vg 0    I[1,0] = (_v1 - VGS0 - Vspan*tanh(Kloop*(Iset - _c1)/Vspan))/Rc    C[1] = IDD

C[1] = IDD makes _c1 the current through the IProbe IDD in the drain supply line. The equation is a source behind Rc = 1 mΩ that holds its node at VGS0 − Vspan·tanh(Kloop·(Idd − Iset)/Vspan): proportional control, Kloop volts of gate per amp of error, with its output held within Vspan = ±1 V of the nominal bias by the tanh, as a real controller is held by its supply rails. The gate is fed from that node through a choke. Bench 5's FET is swept over a threshold shift dVt (added to TV0), with the loop open (Kloop = 0) and closed (200 V/A), at DC:

dVt Idq, open loop Idq, closed loop Gate bias, closed loop
−0.3 V 91.4 mA 62.9 mA −3.34 V
0 61.4 mA 61.4 mA −3.05 V
+0.3 V 41.2 mA 59.9 mA −2.75 V

Open loop, a 0.3 V threshold spread moves the quiescent current by ±50 %; closed, by ±2.5 %, the residual a proportional loop of gain 16 (200 V/A times the device's 80 mA/V) leaves. A larger Kloop holds it tighter. The rails matter: a loop this simple also has a second, unphysical solution, the gate driven so far negative that gate-drain breakdown carries the supply current and the loop pushes the gate further still. With Vspan=1000 the DC solve finds it at dVt = +0.3 V, the gate at −238 V.

Under drive. The bench also runs a harmonic-balance drive sweep, 10 to 30 dBm at 2 GHz, into bench 5's maximum-power load (the LOAD tuner: 63.7 + j17.0 Ω at the drain, its harmonics shorted), with the loop open and closed:

Pin Idd, open loop Idd, closed loop Gate bias, closed loop Pout, open Pout, closed
10 dBm 83.1 mA 62.9 mA −3.29 V 23.7 dBm 21.5 dBm
14 dBm 116.7 mA 65.5 mA −3.59 V 28.9 dBm 24.2 dBm
18 dBm 198.1 mA 80.2 mA −4.05 V 34.7 dBm 27.4 dBm
22 dBm 340.2 mA 204.8 mA −4.05 V 39.7 dBm 35.9 dBm
30 dBm 535.6 mA 480.0 mA −4.05 V 41.8 dBm 41.7 dBm

The RF swing raises a class-AB stage's average current, and this controller answers it instantly: it pushes the gate negative to hold the current near its 61.4 mA target, deeper towards class B, which costs gain (2.1 dB at 10 dBm, 7.4 dB at 18 dBm). At 18 dBm it reaches its rail, VGS0 − Vspan = −4.05 V, and from there the current rises as the open loop's does, and the output meets it again at saturation. A loop filter, a weighting function I[1,2] with H[2]=1/(1+j*2*pi*freq*Tloop), acts at DC and not at the harmonics, so it keeps RF off the controller's output; harmonic balance solves the steady state, where even a slow loop has settled on the average current, so the bias it reaches is the one above. A controller that holds the bias it set before the drive arrives, as a sampled one does between bursts, is a transient question.
