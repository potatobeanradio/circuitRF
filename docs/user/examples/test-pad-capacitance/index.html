Test Pad Capacitance

A test pad on the far side of a six-layer board with no nets, joined to a 50 ohm line through a via with unused inner pads: its capacitance as built and with the pads removed, what goes wrong when the ground planes are left floating, and a schematic showing the electrostatic C reproduce a driven 3D run below the via's resonance.

Open this example

File ▸ New ▸ Examples ▸ Test Pad Capacitance copies this workspace to a folder you choose and opens it on this README. Every example is listed in Example Workspaces.

A test pad on the far side of a six-layer board, reached from a 50 Ω line on top through a via. The via keeps an unused pad in every inner plane it passes, as board CAD does unless told otherwise. How much does the test pad load the line, and how much does removing those pads save? Palace's electrostatic solver answers in under a minute per board. A driven 3D run of the same board then shows where that answer holds.

The board has no nets, like a board imported from Gerber files. Every terminal is chosen by clicking the metal, and the ground by choosing Every body not in a terminal. Capacitance Extraction, the example before this one, introduces the method on a simpler board. The note AN-03 Extracting capacitance (Help ▸ circuitRF Documentation ▸ Application Notes) explains it.

First: Simulate will ask you to install Palace

Opening this workspace needs no solver. The first time you press Simulate on a setup without Palace, the run is refused with Install Palace …. That is the expected first step.

The board

12 × 8 mm of the technology tech/six-layer-fr4.ctech: FR-4 (εr 4.4) in five layers, 1.6 mm in all. No layer is marked as a ground reference, and no piece of copper carries a net.

Layer What is on it
Top Copper the 0.38 mm line, 50 Ω over Inner 1, end to end; a 0.6 mm pad at the via and at each stitching via
Inner 1 – Inner 4 a ground plane each, with a 1.0 mm opening round the via; in As built, a 0.6 mm unused pad on the via inside each opening
Bottom Copper the 1.5 mm test pad on the via, and a ground pour with a 2.5 mm opening round it

A 0.3 mm via joins the line to the test pad, and eight stitching vias join the planes and the pour.

Cell What is different
As built as above
Fixed the four unused pads removed, and Inner 4's opening under the test pad enlarged to 2.5 mm
Line only the via, its pads and the test pad cut away: the line by itself
Compare a schematic of the line with each answer on it, and the driven run

Each board cell holds its layout and a 3D view of it (New 3D View from Layout), and the setups are in the 3D view's Simulate ▸ Setup Analyses….

How the setup was made

  1. Add, then Electrostatic and Palace.
  2. In Terminals press +, name the row TP, press Pick, and click the test pad on the bottom of the board. The row takes the whole piece of metal the pad is part of: the pad, the via, the unused pads, the top pad and the line. They touch, so they are one conductor. The 3D view selects all of it.
  3. Open the Ground list (▾) and choose Every body not in a terminal. The planes, the pour and the stitching vias are ground, and Other metal reads Ground.

Test pad C, planes floating in As built is the same setup with step 3 left out.

Test Pad Capacitance: the numbers

Measured once on an Apple M4 with 10 cores and 16 GB, macOS 27.0, Palace 0.18.1 on 10 processes and Gmsh 4.15.2. A time is the whole run's wall clock and a memory figure is Palace's own peak. The electrostatic setups run at Standard.

Setup Test-pad net Time Peak
As built ▸ Test pad C 2.140 pF 44 s 5.3 GB
Fixed ▸ Test pad C 1.830 pF 48 s 6.0 GB
Line only ▸ Line C 1.450 pF 40 s 5.9 GB

The terminal is the whole net, line included. The test pad's own share is the difference from the line alone:

  • As built: 0.689 pF more than the line alone.
  • Fixed: 0.379 pF, 45 % less.

What a designer does with the number. Put it on the line in the schematic as a shunt capacitor, at the via, and see what it costs at the operating frequency. At 2.4 GHz the as-built pad costs a few tenths of a dB and returns a reflection a matching network has to absorb. If that matters, removing the unused pads and opening the plane under the pad are two fabrication notes, and this is how much they buy.

Ground, or floating

As built ▸ Test pad C, planes floating took 56 s and reads 0.920 pF: less than half. With the ground box left as it opens, the planes are in no terminal and are not ground, so they float: connected to nothing and with no net charge. The only ground left is the metal box round the model, and the floating planes sit between it and the pad. On a real board the planes are ground, so on a board with no nets, choose Every body not in a terminal.

Compare

Compare/schematic/Compare.csch holds five copies of the 12 mm line, each from its own pair of ports. The line is the line calculator's: Z₀ 50 Ω, εeff 3.33, and its loss at 2.4 GHz. The line's middle carries:

Ports On the line
1, 2 C_built, the as-built test pad from the electrostatic solve
3, 4 C_fixed, the fixed one
5, 6 TESTPAD, the closed-form model of the as-built pad, its via and its unused pads
7, 8 the driven 3D run of the as-built board, Compare/data/As built driven.s2p
9, 10 C_built behind L_via, the via inductance TESTPAD reports (0.526 nH)

Simulate it and open Compare.cdd beside this file. It plots |S21| of all five.

At 2.4 GHz the electrostatic shunt C gives −0.44 dB, and the driven run −0.69 dB. Below about half the stub's resonance the electrostatic number stands in for the driven run. Above that the via's inductance takes over: the driven run dips to −29.6 dB at 7.4 GHz, where the stub resonates, and no capacitor can do that. Ports 9 and 10 put the inductance back, and the dip returns at 8.4 GHz, a little higher because TESTPAD's inductance is an estimate.

The driven run is As built ▸ Driven (Draft): Palace at Draft with two refinement passes (first-order elements), 0.5–10 GHz, lumped ports from the line's ends down to Inner 1. It took 7.3 min, with a peak of 7.8 GB. Its file in Compare/data is that run's result, kept so Compare works without it. A new run writes to results/, not there. What Draft trades away: at first order the mesh round a small pad and via is coarse, and a coarse mesh overstates a small capacitor's loading. Draft with no refinement loaded the line about as much as twice the electrostatic stub below 3 GHz. The two refinement passes close most of that gap, and take three to four times as long. A second-order driven run was not made, so how far this one still is from a converged answer has not been measured.

What is deliberately not here

Results, other than the driven run's file in Compare/data. Run the setups yourself.
