Co-simulation: B1, SAR and the radiation pattern from a schematic

Parts as ports. One EM run keeps each port's fields, and a schematic's fields analysis turns the waves its circuit delivers into B1, SAR and the radiation pattern — so tuning, optimizing and yield on fields start no EM run.

An MRI coil is a resonant conductor with many tuning, matching and decoupling capacitors next to lossy tissue. Solving it with the capacitors inside the EM model means a new EM run for every capacitor value, and a high-Q coil rings for a long time in FDTD. The method coil designers use instead is co-simulation: every capacitor gap and every feed is a port, the EM run solves the bare conductors once and keeps each port's fields, and a circuit puts the capacitors back. circuitRF does the second half in a schematic, so B1 and SAR follow every value in it — tuned, optimized or drawn for a yield trial — in about a second and with no EM run.

Research and engineering use

circuitRF's SAR is for research and engineering. It is not a compliance tool.

The worked example is SAR: MRI Loop Coil (File ▸ New ▸ Examples): a 7 T loop with four ports, matched and optimized against S11, B1 and psSAR goals in 4 s. What B1 and SAR are, and their units, is SAR and B1. An antenna board with parts on it works the same way: see The radiation pattern, and the Antenna Co-Simulation example.

1. The EM side: ports for capacitors, fields kept

In the coil's 3D view:

  1. Put a lumped port across every gap a capacitor will bridge, and one at each feed. Give them all the same Z0 (50 Ω): the Touchstone records one reference impedance, so a block whose ports differ would be read in the wrong one.
  2. In the openEMS setup's SAR and B1 group, set the frequency, the masses and B1 as usual, and tick Keep per-port fields (PerPortFields).
  3. Simulate. openEMS runs once per port, as any driven run does. Beside the .sNp the run writes a result record, <stem>.cemr, and a field set, a folder <stem>.fields/: each port's E and H over the tissue's box, per √W incident at that port and nothing incident anywhere else, with the cells' σ, ρ and volume and a manifest. They are float32, 24 bytes a cell per port and field; the loop coil's four ports at 298 MHz take 87 MB on the example's grid. The disk estimate before the run says how much of it the field set is.

Without capacitors there is no resonance to ring down. The example's four runs stop after about 3,700–3,900 steps each; the same coil with lumped capacitors in the EM model needed 72,000 steps for one run, and it was still ringing.

2. The schematic side: the fields analysis

Place an EM Field Data block in a test bench and pick the run's .cemr for its File. Its pins are named after the run's ports, in the run's order, so there is no port count to set. Draw the circuit around them: the capacitors, the match, the feed. Then add a Fields (SAR, B1, pattern) analysis in the Analysis editor. Its Block control lists the schematic's EM Field Data blocks, those inside sub-cells included, and its Frequency control lists what the block's record stores, with All when it holds a far field. Both still take typed text. Per watt and Averaged SAR are grey when the record holds no field set, since they act only on SAR and B1:

analysis F1 type=fields block=EM1 freq=298 freqUnit=MHz drive=1 [norm=accepted|available] [average=yes|no]
Key
block the EM Field Data block's instance name (X1.EM1 inside a cell) required
freq, freqUnit a frequency the record stores, or all for every frequency of its far field required
drive the circuit port that drives; every other circuit port is terminated in its Z0 required
weights with several drives, each one's complex amplitude (multi-channel) 1 each
norm per watt accepted at the drive, or per watt available from it accepted
average no skips the cube-averaged SAR, the step that costs the most yes

Simulate solves the circuit once at that frequency, takes the incident wave the circuit delivers into each of the block's ports, sums the stored fields with those weights, and computes SAR and B1 from the sum exactly as a 3D run does. Fields are combined before SAR is formed, never SAR itself. The results land beside the field set, in <stem>.fields/cosim/<analysis>/; the Messages pane's Open in 3D view opens the coil's 3D view with the recombined result drawn and selected. Its solution is labelled <f> GHz, fields 'F1' (circuit port 1 driven).

The Messages pane gives the run one line for the pattern's notes, what each metric means and why any was not published, with Details… to read them. A check that fails is a warning of its own, and so is a drive port that delivers no power to the block, which leaves the results empty. A frequency the block's record does not store is flagged under the Frequency control and refused if you run it.

A fields analysis has no Simulate by choice, because the block already holds the EM result. On one, model=em and --em on start no EM run of the schematic's layout; they switch only EM-capable parts the circuit places, as a part set to EM is.

Per watt available or accepted. With norm=accepted (the default) the numbers are per watt that entered the circuit at the drive, so a poor match does not show: B1 per √W accepted barely moves as the match changes. With norm=available they are per watt the source could deliver, so a poor match shows as a weak B1, as it would on the scanner. The loop coil example uses available.

What the run checks, each a note, and a warning when it fails:

  • the summed field's absorbed power against the field set's own tissue matrix, to 0.1 %;
  • the power the circuit delivers into the block against the block's own S-matrix, to 2 %: this catches a .sNp read between its points, or one that is not the run's;
  • that no excitation absorbs more than it dissipates.

All three compare the field set with itself. They cannot see a field that is wrong in the same way in E and in S — a grid too coarse, a run stopped too early. The checks hold the recombination to its inputs; the inputs are the 3D run's.

The block works on the saved result. It reads the record and the files the record names, and no .c3d, .cem or .clay, so editing the setup, or deleting the 3D view, changes nothing it uses. Its properties show one line from the record: when, which solver and quality, the sweep and the port count. Run settings… beside it shows the settings that run used, read-only, with what differs from the setup now marked. A difference adds one note to a run and blocks nothing. A record from before settings were kept reads settings not recorded.

What the block refuses, naming itself and the file: no such record; a record holding neither a far field nor a field set, whose S-parameters belong in an SnP; a file the record names that is missing or truncated; and files from different runs, saying which. The record, the .sNp, the .npy and the field set share one stamp. File times are not compared, so a copied or checked-out result reads as current.

A Touchstone file is not enough. A Fields analysis on a plain SnP is refused. A run from before records has a .sNp and a .npy and no .cemr: pick its .sNp in the block's file picker and it offers to write one beside it, as circuitrf em --adopt does, with the data files untouched. A saved schematic with a Fields analysis on an SnP still opens; running it says so, and the Messages pane offers Change to EM Field Data when the block's .sNp can be adopted.

Headless: circuitrf fields bench.csch runs every enabled fields analysis of a schematic (-a names some, --set changes a value first) and prints the same lines; MCP's run analysis=fields does the same.

3. Tuning and optimizing on fields

Seven functions read a fields analysis, wherever a measure or goal expression is written. F is the analysis's name.

Function Value Per
B1pMean(F, region) volume-weighted mean |B1+| over the region T per √W
B1pMin(F, region) the smallest |B1+| there T per √W
B1pCoV(F, region) the spread of |B1+| over its mean (a surface coil's brain reads about 1) —
psSAR(F, mass) peak spatial-average SAR of mass grams, the full cube average W/kg per W
SARwb(F, region) the region's absorbed power over its mass W/kg per W
SarEff(F, region, mass) B1pMean / √psSAR T per √(W/kg)
Pabs(F) the power absorbed in all tissue W per W

"Per W" is the analysis's own norm. A region is a string: omitted, the setup's own (B1 mean over for B1, Region for SAR); "all", every tissue; a tissue's path ("head/brain"), its last part ("brain"), a phantom's name ("head") or a material; + joins several ("skin+skull"). "box(x0, x1, y0, y1, z0, z1)" takes every cell whose centre lies inside, each number with a length unit; B1 is taken over all of them (air too), SAR over the tissue. An unknown word is refused, naming the tissues the field set has.

goal Match  = dB(SP1.S(1,1)) analysis=SP1 over=freq lo=298 MHz hi=298 MHz le -20
goal B1     = 1e6 * B1pMean(F1, "brain") analysis=F1 ge 0.2
goal SAR10g = psSAR(F1, 10) analysis=F1 le 2

The Goal editor lists all seven under Fields (SAR, B1) for each fields analysis. Each evaluation does only what its caller needs:

  • The optimizer, yield, centering and DOE solve the circuit and form the waves; Pabs is then exact from the tissue matrix, and B1 and SARwb read only their region's cells. Nothing is written.
  • psSAR is always the full cube average, never a sample or a coarser grid standing in for it, so an evaluation that scores it recombines the whole field set. On the loop coil example a whole evaluation, average included, is about a tenth of a second; the run's notes name the goals that pay it.
  • A tuning slider moving rewrites local SAR and B1, so an open 3D view's plot follows the slider; psSAR waits until the slider is released, and says so rather than show a stale value.
  • Simulate and the optimizer's final point do everything and write it.

No openEMS process starts in any of it. Yield trials, the optimizer and the sliders all recombine the stored fields. A re-run trial (run analysis=yield trial=<n>, or the Data Display's re-run) is written under its own label, F1 (trial n), beside the analysis's own results.

4. Several channels

A coil with several feeds — a quadrature birdcage, a transmit array — is driven by naming several circuit ports and an amplitude for each:

W2 = polar(1,-90)
analysis F1 type=fields block=EM1 freq=298 freqUnit=MHz drive=1,2 weights=1,W2
tune mag(W2) min=0.5 max=2 opt=1
tune phase(W2) min=-180 deg max=0 deg opt=1

The circuit is solved once with every drive at its weight. Per watt then means per watt of the drives' total, accepted or available, so "maximise B1pMin with psSAR at most so much per W total" is an ordinary optimizer problem: a channel's amplitude and phase are the parts of a complex variable, and ordinary tunables.

5. The radiation pattern

A 3D run with component ports stores a pattern per port, and so does a planar run. A fields analysis combines them for the parts in the schematic, so the pattern of the antenna as built follows every part value with no new EM run.

  1. In the 3D setup, tick the parts in Component ports, turn on Radiation pattern and tick Create SnP schematic when the run finishes.
  2. Simulate. The run makes a cell named after the setup with _cosim on the end, and writes the same circuit as <setup>_cosim.cnl. The cell holds an EM Field Data block on the run's record, a Port for each of the 3D view's own ports and each connector, and each part from its port's node to ground, with its model from the board's schematic. A part with no model there is a placeholder variable, named in a note on the sheet and by check.
  3. Set the values and simulate the cell. It has an S-parameter analysis over the sweep and analysis F1 type=fields block=BOARD drive=1 at the stored frequency nearest the sweep's centre, with a note naming every stored frequency. Its Data Display holds S11, a polar cut of F1.farfield.U and the realized gain.
  4. Change values with a tuning slider, a parametric sweep, --set, the optimizer or yield. None starts an EM run. The Fields analysis's S is at its own frequencies, so F1.S lines up with the pattern.

A cell of that name is never overwritten: edit it freely and run the setup again, and the block reads the new result. A run that kept neither a far field nor a field set gets an SnP in place of the block. A run with no component ports makes the _sim cell described under A schematic to start from.

The worked example is Antenna Co-Simulation (File ▸ New ▸ Examples): a meander antenna solved once at five frequencies, then matched by the optimizer, with its pattern compared across the band and against an inductor's error.

To wire a circuit by hand, place an EM Field Data block as in The schematic side and add a Fields analysis. RealizedGainDbi(F1), GainDbi, DirectivityDbi, RadiationEfficiency, TrpDbm and PeakEirpDbm read the pattern in a measure or goal line.

A planar result

A planar run with Radiation pattern on works the same way: place an EM Field Data block on its record and add a Fields analysis. The run makes no _cosim cell, since component ports are built only in a 3D view, so the block's pins are the setup's own ports and the circuit is yours to draw. The hemisphere stays a hemisphere.

  • A port for a part is an Internal delta gap. Draw the part's site as continuous metal, put a port on it and set its type to Internal delta gap in the EM setup's port list. Two pads with a real gap between them cannot be a port, because a delta gap cuts metal. An Internal port goes down a via to the plane, and any via in the structure stops the run producing a pattern.
  • An edge port is recombined at its reference plane. A circuit on it sees the de-embedded S, and the run keeps what relates that plane to the port's gap. The pattern still holds the current on any feed the solver grew.
  • Gain and efficiency read as the run's own do. With nothing around the block they are the numbers the run wrote. With parts around it, what the parts dissipate is added to what the structure takes, and realized gain includes the mismatch at the circuit's drive.
  • Three power terms are omitted by name: PowerSurfaceWave, PowerConductor with PowerFilm, and PowerDielectricAndGround. Each is quadratic in the currents, so a port's number from the run cannot be added up for a circuit. PowerAccepted and PowerRadiated are published, and their difference is every loss together.
  • Refused: ports in a calibration group, and a result from before this was kept, unless every port is an internal gap. Run it again.

What can be placed across a gap

The N-port has one common reference, but a gap port's negative end is a pad, not ground. So where a part goes depends on the port's kind:

On Place Because
a gap port a part from the port's node to ground the circuit's ground stands for that port's own pad, so the part bridges the pads
two gap ports not a part between their nodes it is in series with both ports' references, which is not the board; check warns, naming the part and both ports
a feed, or any port with a real return any network, series or shunt its reference is the board's real return

A part with more pads connects each signal pin to its port's node and its ground pin to ground.

A two-port file on a gap port. A two-port part file goes with port 1 on the node and port 2 on ground, so its series impedance stands across the gap. Any shunt branch in the file does not reach the board's ground plane: port 1's stands across the gap, and port 2's is shorted out. A file whose pad-to-ground capacitance matters therefore shows it in the wrong place. The pads themselves are in the 3D model, and so is their capacitance to the plane.

The part's body is not in the 3D model, with this method or any other that uses a gap port.

A part not fitted. A part whose Disable is Open in the board's schematic leaves its port open in the generated netlist and cell, and one set to Short shorts its port. Neither gets a placeholder value.

Frequencies

A pattern exists only at frequencies the run solved. Far-field phase across a coarse sweep is not safe to interpolate, so a frequency between stored points is refused, listing them. freq=all takes every stored one. S comes from the same result, so a denser S-parameter analysis in the same schematic reads between the Touchstone's points as any SnP does, while its pattern stays at the stored frequencies.

6. Working from saved results

The block reads a result on disk, so a result outlives its setup: the setup can be edited, the 3D view deleted or the workspace moved, and the block reads what it read.

  • A run that does not finish changes no result. Results are staged and published only once the run has finished, so a failed, cancelled or refused run leaves the previous .sNp, .npy, field set and record as they were, and a schematic reading them goes on working. A solver's working files are not part of this: a run that starts clears them, so 3D field plots of the previous run can be gone after one that fails.
  • A run that finishes replaces the result of its name. The setup's Output name decides that name for every file, so a new name keeps the earlier result.
  • A result's settings can be read back. Run settings… on the block, or circuitrf explain <name>.cemr, shows what that run used and what differs from the setup now.

Costs and limits

Lumped capacitors in the EM model Co-simulation
EM one run per capacitor value; the loop coil's took 72,000 steps one run per port, a few thousand steps each, once
a capacitor changed a new EM run one circuit solve and a recombination, about a second, no process
  • Agreement. On the loop coil, on the tutorial's own grid, the co-simulation reproduces the lumped-capacitor run to +1.5 % in B1 and +3.0 % in psSAR10g. About four fifths of that is the lumped run's own ring-down cut short: run to −60 dB it moves to within 0.3 % in B1 and 0.6 % in psSAR10g of the co-simulation.
  • One frequency per field set, the SAR frequency: a fields analysis at any other is refused.
  • A parametric sweep wraps a fields analysis over a far field and adds its axis to every cube. Make the sweep's axis the Family of a pattern cut for one curve per value, each named in the legend, and plot F1.farfield.RealizedGainDbi along it for the peak. The run summary states the memory its cubes take and warns above 2 GB, when naming one frequency in place of freq=all is the remedy. It is refused at a frequency with a field set: SAR and B1 volumes are written per run and the cube average is a long solve per run, so those stay a slider's or --set's, one value at a time. A result holding both still sweeps its pattern at freq=all.
  • How far the pattern agrees. Measured on a 50 mm strip dipole at 3 GHz with a feed and two gap ports, against a run with those parts in the EM model or those loads on the ports, on one mesh and within 20 dB of the peak. Palace and openEMS agree to 0.003 dB or better in peak gain and pattern, and within 2e-4 in S11, with the parts 5 nH and 1 pF or the loads 200 Ω and 100 Ω. EMerge at Standard reads 0.043 dB in peak gain, 0.079 dB in pattern and 3.8e-3 in S11. Its gain and pattern are within what the method asks, and its S11 across a change of load needs a fine port edge mesh: an EdgeRefinement of 0.02 gives 7.5e-4.
  • Near a resonance (6.5 nH and 0.05 pF on the same dipole), Palace agrees to 0.009 dB and 1.6e-3 in S11 on its own mesh. Draft and Standard disagree with each other by far more than that: these comparisons show the method is right, not that the mesh is. A radiating dipole is a mild case, and a sharper resonance has not been measured.
  • The air box moves gain and efficiency more than S11. On a 95 mm board at 868 MHz with first-order Palace elements, padding of 86, 130 and 170 mm read efficiency 100.5, 90.9 and 84.9 % and gain 2.09, 1.51 and 1.25 dBi, while S11 stayed within 0.1 dB and directivity within 0.15 dB. λ/4 passed the run's own power balance and was still too small. Check a pattern's gain at a second box size.
  • A cylindrical grid's field set recombines as a Cartesian one does, but its volumes are not drawn in the 3D view.
